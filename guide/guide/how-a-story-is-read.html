How a story is read ​

Every view of a story (the plot grid, the threads, the Graph, the manuscript) is a reading of the same files. How this story is read is the page that shows that reading on your own story: for each rule Leathercase reads by, what it found in your folder, and a fix where something looks off. This page explains each part of it, each question it can ask, and what each fix does.

A fix writes only Leathercase's own files, such as an ignore pattern in the story's settings or a note Leathercase owns, or it opens your note at the right spot for you to change. Your notes are never written.

Opening it ​

How this story is read is a page about the story rather than a view of it. Open it from:

  • the breadcrumb's view menu (the view's name with a small arrow), below the views;
  • the ⋯ on the open story's row in the sidebar, or the breadcrumb's story menu;
  • How this story is read in the View menu, or typed in the find field;
  • N unsorted, in amber, in a view's state line;
  • Why? See how this story is read, in a view that found nothing to show;
  • Show me on the notice about a story declared before roles;
  • How it is read, beside Files on the story's settings page.

The breadcrumb then reads Stories › The Lamp at Ferrol › How this story is read. Leaving it, by choosing a view, goes back to the view you were on.

The state line and the side column ​

The state line at the top sums up the reading: 14 notes read · 32 scenes written · nothing looks off, or 1 thing looks off, 3 things look off. While the folder is read it says reading….

The side column says what the page is: Each rule Leathercase reads a story by, with what it found in this folder. A fix writes only Leathercase's own files, or opens your note at the spot. and What Leathercase read from your folder, as it found it. Nothing here is written back. The patterns that choose these files live in Story settings. Its button, The shape of a story folder, opens the guide's page on laying out a story folder (see the end of this page).

Each rule below is a section with its title, a count beside it, a line that states the rule, and what it found. Long lists scroll within their box. Wherever a file is listed with Open, the button opens it in your writing software.

What looks off ​

The first section lists everything that needs you, each with an amber dot and its fix. When there is nothing it says Nothing looks off. Each rule below says what it found. When no file matched the story's patterns at all it says No note matched the story's patterns. Check the folder and the patterns in Story settings. with Open story settings.

A story declared before roles ​

Leathercase now reads as scenes only the files you declare as prose (see the seven roles). A story declared before that change still reads the old way, where whatever nothing claimed was prose, and opening a story never writes anything, so the story keeps its old reading until you say.

The notice says how many notes are read as scenes today, and where. When some would stop being scenes, it says so: 2 notes will no longer be read as scenes: Ideas/Sea.md, Symbols/Lamp.md. When nothing is read as prose yet, it says Leathercase now reads as scenes only the prose you declare. Nothing in this story is read as prose yet.

Keep this reading writes today's prose into the story's Story.json as declared. From then on a file added where nothing claims it waits, unsorted, instead of becoming a scene. The notice comes back each time until you keep the reading.

Questions about unsorted files ​

A file is unsorted when nobody has said what it is, or when its type: is a word the story does not know. It is never a scene. Leathercase groups unsorted files and asks one question per group:

  • by type: 2 notes say type: symbol. What are they?
  • by folder: 3 notes in Ideas/ are not sorted. What does the folder hold?
  • at the top: 1 note at the top of the story's folder is not sorted. What is it?

Beside the question, Choose what they are… opens the answers:

AnswerOffered forWhat it does
Things that recur, a kind of their ownEvery groupMakes a kind of your own, found in the prose by its names. A Named field appears, filled from the type word or the folder's name, such as Symbol or Clue.
Things that recur, found by their images too (symbols, motifs)Every groupMakes a kind of your own of the symbol quality, also found by the images you list for each note. Named appears too.

Characters, Places, Items, Factions, EventsA folder, or the top of the storyThe folder holds that kind

A kind of your own, by nameEvery group, once the story has someThe files join it

Nothing is chosen until you choose, so Answer never writes a guess; it stays greyed out until you pick. Answer writes your answer into the story's Story.json. One answer settles every file in the group, and every file written later that says the same type or sits in the same folder.

Don't ask again leaves the files unsorted and never asks about that group again. They still count in the state line's N unsorted.

A kind of your own appears afterwards in the Graph, counted in the shape of the story, in the Tags view under Kinds of your own, and in the folder selects of the story's settings. See Kinds of your own.

Renames, and names Leathercase's files still use ​

When you rename a character's note in your writing app, Leathercase asks whether it is a rename, here and over the story's views. Yes, follow Nora Vidal carries Leathercase's own files to the new name; No, they are different is never asked again. When one of Leathercase's files names someone no note answers to, the finding offers to open that file at the line, or the story's settings when the name is in Story.json. The full account is on Homes and stories.

Work read as story ​

Memos, research and reviews talk about the cast in ways that are not scenes. When notes read as prose sit in a folder whose name starts with _ (such as _Work), or in a folder named for work about the story (Work, Memos, Research, Reviews, Archive, Templates, Trash, and the same words in the other languages Leathercase reads), Leathercase says so.

Leave _Work/** out adds the pattern _Work/** to the files the story leaves out, in its settings. The story is read again: the memos leave the Graph, the grid, the threads and the manuscript, and they are listed under Notes left out as left out by _Work/**. The memo's file is unchanged. A pattern already in the story's settings is never offered twice. To take it back, remove the pattern under Advanced: the patterns in Story settings.

A type and a folder that disagree ​

A note in Places/ whose properties say type: character is read as a character: its type wins over its folder. Leathercase points it out in case one of the two is a mistake. Open the note at its type opens the note in your writing software at the line that says its type, so you can change whichever is wrong.

A long note with no heading ​

Leathercase splits prose into scenes only at headings. A prose note of 1,500 words or more with no heading is read as one scene, which usually means a whole chapter is one row on the grid. Open the note opens it, so you can give each scene its own heading. Leathercase never splits at --- or blank lines.

A name with no note ​

A name the prose uses eight times or more with no note of its own is raised here, up to five of them, the most named first; every other such name is listed under Names without a note.

  • Make a character note makes a note for the name, owned by Leathercase, in the story's suite folder, and opens it in the Graph for you to fill.
  • Make a place note does the same as a place.

The note is Leathercase's file, not yours: nothing is added to your own folders.

Notes typed as the cast ​

A note is a character, place, item, faction or event by the type in its properties, or by the folder it sits in. The type wins.

Each typed note is listed by name, with its kind and how it was typed: character · by its type, place · by its folder, or character · by what you said its folder holds. When there are none: No note is typed yet. Keep character notes in a Characters folder, or write type: character in a note's properties.

Notes read as story ​

Only the notes declared as the story's prose. Their headings split them into scenes; a note with prose and no heading is one scene.

Each prose note is listed by its path, with what it gave: 3 scenes, 4 links, 2 relationships, or no heading: read as one scene, and Open. When there are none: No note is read as story yet.

A file nothing claims is not story; it waits under Unsorted until you say what it is.

Plans, records, work notes, research and kinds of your own ​

Notes about the story that are not its prose: they never make scenes. Their own type: line or their folder says what they are.

Each is listed by path with its role, such as Plans (outline, synopsis), Research or the name of your own kind, and Open. When there are none: None.

Unsorted ​

Files nothing claims, or whose type: the story does not know. They are never scenes. Say what a folder holds above, or in Story settings.

Each is listed by path with unsorted, or unsorted · type: symbol when it gives a type the story does not know, and Open. When there are none: None: every file is sorted.

Other names ​

Names the prose may use for someone with a note: from the note's aliases, or confirmed in the Graph and kept in Story settings.

Each is listed as Nora is Nora Vidal, with where it comes from: the note (its aliases: property) or Story settings (a name you confirmed in the Graph). When there are none: No other names yet. See Graph and Front matter.

Names without a note ​

Names the prose uses three times or more with no note of their own. The Graph shows them dashed; a fix above makes a note for the ones named most.

Each is listed with its count, such as 5 mentions. When there are none: Every name the prose uses often has a note. In the Graph you can say what such a name is, another name for someone, or not a name at all.

The grid file ​

The plot grid is its own file, PlotGrid.md, made from the story the first time it was read: the scenes as rows, linked to the story's files, and the scenes the writer planned. It replaced Outline.md, whose planned scenes it carried in.

It shows the file's path and every planned scene is written, or 2 scenes planned, not yet written. Before the grid has opened once: No grid file yet. It is made the first time the plot grid opens. In practice the file is made the first time any of the story's views reads it, since one reading serves the grid, the threads and the Graph. The file is PlotGrid.md in the story's suite/ folder; New scene, Plan a scene and beat cards sent to the grid write their planned scenes into it, and Outline.md is never written. See Plot grid.

The shape of a story folder ​

At the foot of the page, and in the side column, The shape of a story folder opens the guide's one page inside the app, in the app's language: the folder layout Leathercase reads best, shown on the sample story, the rules it reads by, a smaller shape for a short story, and what the reading cannot do (it splits scenes only at headings; it reads she and he back to the nearest name, but not an epithet; it reads Markdown, Word, Scrivener and Fountain; see Files Leathercase reads). Back to how this story is read returns; Try the sample opens the sample library. The same page is offered at first run, under Wondering how Leathercase will read your folder?

The questions over the story's views ​

You do not have to open this page to be asked. Over every view of the story, a strip carries the same questions, one at a time:

  1. first the notice about a story declared before roles, with Keep this reading, Show me (which opens this page) and Later;
  2. then the unsorted groups, the largest first, with the answers above, Don't ask again and Later, and how many more wait, such as 2 more;
  3. then, in a strip of its own, a rename waiting, with Yes, follow … and No, they are different.

Later hides the strip until Leathercase is opened again. The strips are not shown on this page or on the story's settings page, where the same questions are listed in full.
