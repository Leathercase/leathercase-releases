Getting started ​

This page takes you from the download to your first ten minutes in Leathercase. It covers installing the app on macOS, Windows and Linux, the first-run screens that pick your writing home, the sample library you can try before pointing Leathercase at your own files, and the settings that switch things off.

Leathercase reads your files where they already are. It never edits your manuscript: everything it adds is its own small files, in markdown and JSON, in a folder you choose.

Download ​

Every version is published on the releases page: github.com/Leathercase/leathercase-releases/releases. Open the newest release and download the file for your system from its list of assets.

SystemFile to downloadUpdates itself
macOS, Apple silicon (M1 and later)leathercase-<version>-mac-arm64.dmgYes
macOS, Intelleathercase-<version>-mac-x64.dmgYes
Windows (x64 and Arm)leathercase-<version>-win.exeYes
Linux, AppImageleathercase-<version>-linux-x86_64.AppImage, or the arm64 oneYes
Linux, Debian and Ubuntu packageleathercase-<version>-linux-amd64.deb, or the arm64 oneNo: Leathercase opens the download page instead

The .zip files beside the macOS installers and the latest*.yml files are for the updater. You do not need them. The SHA256SUMS files let you check a download if you want to.

"Updates itself" means that when you ask Leathercase to check for a newer version, it can download it and install it on restart. Nothing is checked or downloaded unless you ask. See Updates and uninstalling.

Install ​

macOS ​

  1. Open the .dmg you downloaded.
  2. In the window that opens, drag Leathercase onto Applications, following the arrow.
  3. Open it from Applications or Launchpad.

The Mac app is signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens without a warning. It needs macOS 12 or later.

Windows ​

  1. Run leathercase-<version>-win.exe.
  2. Windows SmartScreen may warn that the publisher is unknown: the Windows installer is not signed yet. Choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. There is nothing to choose: a small window shows the install's progress, then Leathercase opens. It installs for you alone, needs no administrator rights, and adds Leathercase to the Start menu and the desktop.

Linux: AppImage ​

  1. Make the file executable: right-click it, open Properties, and tick Allow executing file as program, or run chmod +x leathercase-*.AppImage in a terminal.
  2. Double-click it to run.

The AppImage is the Linux build that can update itself.

Linux: .deb ​

  1. Install it with your software centre, or with sudo apt install ./leathercase-<version>-linux-amd64.deb.
  2. Open Leathercase from your applications menu.

A .deb cannot replace itself. When a newer version is out, Leathercase opens its download page and you install the new .deb the same way.

From a version before 0.30 ​

Before 0.30 the app had another name. On Windows and Linux it updates to 0.30 in place, as any update does. On macOS, download 0.30 once by hand and drag it into Applications, then move the old app to the Trash: the app's identity changed with its name, so the old app cannot replace itself with the new one. Later versions update themselves again. On every system your homes and settings carry over: Leathercase moves them to its own folder the first time it opens.

The first run ​

The first time Leathercase opens it has no writing home yet, so it shows the first-run screens instead of the usual window. Nothing is written to your disk until the last step.

Welcome ​

The welcome screen offers two ways in. Click one to choose it; it turns vermilion.

CardTitleWhat it does
I have a draftRead my manuscriptFor a story you have already written. The card says: From an Obsidian vault, a markdown folder, Scrivener, Word or Fountain. Your files are read, never changed.
I'm starting outStart from ideasFor a story that does not exist yet. The card says: Begin with a story of its own. The grid and threads grow as you add to it.

Press Continue to go on with the card you chose, or click Try the sample story first to skip all of this and open the sample library (see below). The line at the foot says the promise plainly: nothing leaves this computer.

The app speaks the language of your system, matched to the nearest of the three it carries (English, Brazilian Portuguese, European Portuguese). First run does not ask. You can pick another language later in Settings, Appearance; see Languages.

Step 1: Home ​

The steps are numbered across the top: 1 Home, 2 Stories, 3 Open in for a draft, or 1 Home, 2 First story when you start from ideas.

Where do you keep your stories? Your writing home is one folder that holds your stories. Leathercase keeps its own files there too, so whatever syncs that folder (Dropbox, Google Drive, iCloud Drive, OneDrive) carries both.

  1. Press Choose… and pick the folder in your system's dialog. The box shows Reading the folder…, then the folder's path.
  2. Under it, Leathercase says what it found:
    • Who carries the folder: A local folder; no sync tool holds it, or Dropbox syncs this folder with a detected badge. Leathercase reads this from the folder's path only. It never uploads anything itself.
    • What is in it: 2 stories found, No story found yet, or Already a writing home: 3 stories when you pick a folder Leathercase has used before, on this or another computer. When stories are found, the line under the count says what counts as one: Folders of markdown, vaults, Word and Fountain files, and Scrivener projects.
  3. Wondering how Leathercase will read your folder? The shape of a story folder opens a short guide page, with Back to return and Try the sample to open the sample instead.
  4. What will be added lists exactly what Leathercase will write:
    • + Suite.md: marks this folder as a writing home.
    • + suite/: the home's own files.
    • + Writer/: your library of references and ideas, made only when you make a card.
    • + one suite/ folder per story: its settings, grid, threads and graph.
  5. Press Use this folder. If the folder is already a writing home and you came by I have a draft, the button reads Open this home and opens it straight away with its stories as they were left.

Some folders cannot be a home, and Leathercase says why in rose under the box: the root of a drive, your whole home folder, a system folder, or the folder where Leathercase keeps its own data. Choose the folder inside that holds your stories.

Step 2: Stories (I have a draft) ​

These look like stories lists every story Leathercase found in the folder: folders of markdown, vaults, Word files, Scrivener projects and Fountain screenplays. Each row shows a tick box, the story's name, its folder (or this folder for the home itself), its source (Markdown, Obsidian, Word, Scrivener, Fountain) and how many files it has.

Untick anything that is not a story. Each ticked story gets its own suite/ folder. A few are offered unticked, with the reason under the list:

  • A folder that holds a single Word file: tick it to read it as a story.
  • Files at the top of the folder, beside story folders: tick them to read them as a story of their own.

The line beside the button counts what you ticked, for example 3 stories · read-only. Press Continue.

When nothing was found, the step says No stories here yet and offers Start from ideas instead.

Step 3: Open in (I have a draft) ​

Where do you write each story? When you select a passage in Leathercase, Open in takes you back to the app you write in, at that spot. This step picks that app for each story.

  • A dashed grey dot means Leathercase inferred the app, and says why: Inferred from the vault (Obsidian), Inferred from .scriv (Scrivener), or The only app here that opens .md.
  • An amber dot means several apps on this computer open that format, for example 3 apps open .md files. Pick one. Choose one from the menu.
  • Opens in your default app means the system's default is used.
  • A vermilion dot means Your choice.

You can change this later, per story, in Settings, Sources. Press Open the Library. Leathercase now writes Suite.md and the suite/ folders, declares the ticked stories and opens the Library.

Step 2: First story (I'm starting out) ​

Name the story. Type a Working title (the field suggests The Salt Road) and press Start the story. Leathercase makes the home's files and a folder named for the story inside your home, and opens the story on its Plot grid. Write the prose in any app you like; Leathercase reads what lands in that folder.

You don't have to start a story when the folder already holds one. If it is already a writing home, from another computer or an earlier install, the step says so, and Skip and open the home opens it as it is. If it is not a home yet but Leathercase found stories in it, Read the stories found goes on to the Stories step instead, as if you had chosen Read my manuscript.

Back, at the foot of every step, returns to the step before.

The sample library ​

Try the sample story first opens a library Leathercase carries inside the app. It is a copy, made fresh in the app's own data folder, never in a folder of yours, so you can change anything in it and lose nothing.

It holds four short stories, one in each format Leathercase reads:

StoryFormatWhat it shows
The Lamp at FerrolMarkdownA mini novel in ten chapters: typed notes for the cast, places, items, factions and events, a hand-written threads note (Story threads.md, the older name, read until Leathercase's own suite/StoryThreads.md exists), an older Outline.md whose planned scene the plot grid carries into its own file, suite/PlotGrid.md, and work notes left out. Every view has something to show.
The Salt RoadWordA novella in four .docx chapters, each scene a Heading 1, with folders of character and place notes.
Orchard HoursScrivenerA Scrivener 3 project with three chapter folders and a discarded scene Leathercase leaves out.
The Night SleeperFountainA short film in one .fountain screenplay.

The sample also has six cards on the Writer Board and three described tags in Tags.

Start here ​

While the sample is open, a column called Start here sits beside the Library. A dashed note at its top reminds you that this is the sample and none of it is yours. Below it are six things to do once, each ticked by doing it, never by a checkbox:

  1. Plot grid
  2. Manuscript
  3. Threads
  4. Find a story, a view or a command
  5. Beat board
  6. Declare a story

The count at the top reads 0 of 6, then 1 of 6 and so on. The next step to do has a Show me button that runs it for you. Under each step is its key, when it has one.

Hide puts Start here away. It stays in the find field: click the field in the title bar with nothing typed and pick Start here to bring it back.

Start over and Leave the sample ​

At the foot of Start here, and in the home switcher at the top of the sidebar while the sample is open:

  • Start over (Start the sample over in the switcher) throws the copy away and opens a fresh one, every change undone.
  • Leave the sample forgets the sample's home and deletes its copy, with nothing left behind. If it was your only home, first run comes back so you can choose your own folder.

Your first ten minutes ​

A walk through The Lamp at Ferrol, from the sample's Library. Each step names the page of this guide that covers it in full. Keys are written for Windows and Linux; on macOS use Cmd in place of Ctrl.

  1. Open the story. In the Library, click The Lamp at Ferrol. The story opens on its Manuscript, and in the sidebar it expands into its views. The title bar shows when the story was read and from how many files. See A tour of the window.
  2. Read the manuscript. Scroll through Act I. The chapters are joined into one book, and the breadcrumb follows the part and chapter at the top of the page. Pick a lens from the footer, such as the rhythm of sentence lengths. See Manuscript and Lenses and style checks.
  3. Open the plot grid with Ctrl+4. Each row is a scene, read from the # headings of the chapters; the columns show who is in it, the point of view, the time and the threads. A row with no prose is a scene planned and not yet written. See Plot grid.
  4. Open the threads with Ctrl+5. The arc of Nora Vidal, the theme What do we owe the drowned? and the subplots run across the scenes as lanes. See Threads.
  5. Open the Graph with Ctrl+6. Every name of the story is there, in groups found from the ties. Point at Nora Vidal to read her card, then drag from the ring around her name to another to tie them. See Graph.
  6. Look at a character. Choose Characters in the sidebar, then Nora Vidal: her voice, the scenes she is in, and her sections. See Characters.
  7. Try the beat board with Ctrl+9: four beat cards the sample already holds, in the columns of a structure template. See Beat board.
  8. Find anything with Ctrl+K. Type ledger and see the item, the scene and the card that match. Type character: Marta and the last row offers to make a note for her. Press Esc to close. See Find, open and make.
  9. See how the story is read. Open the breadcrumb's view menu (the view's name with a small arrow) and choose How this story is read. It lists what Leathercase took as the cast, as prose and as left out, and why. See How a story is read.
  10. Go back to the home. Press Ctrl+2 for the Desk, what changed across all four stories today, then Ctrl+3 for the Writer Board, where the sample's six cards sit.

When you are done, choose Leave the sample and point Leathercase at your own folder, or keep the sample as a second home beside yours.

Turning things off ​

Leathercase does little by itself, and most of what it does can be switched off. Each of these is a preference of this computer, kept in the app's own data and never in your writing home.

WhatDefaultWhere to change it
Checking for a newer versionOff. Leathercase goes online only when you press Check now.Settings, Updates, Check when Leathercase opens
Keys that work from any app (Capture to Writer, Show Leathercase)OffSettings, Hotkeys, Turn on global keys
The mate's heartbeat: looking at a story after a save and every half hourOn. It reads on this computer and wakes no model.Settings, The mate, Heartbeat
The mate's next steps: one suggestion at a time from commands not tried yetOnSettings, The mate, Next steps
The mate's notificationsIn the app only, never a soundSettings, The mate, Notifications
Waking a modelOff, and the app offers no model yetSettings, The mate, When it may wake a model
The assistant's layer: what agents proposed, drawn over the viewsOnCtrl+., or Show or hide the assistant's layer in the View menu
Style checks tinted by the style lensAll eight kinds onSettings, Lenses, Style checks
FadesOnSettings, Accessibility, Reduce motion
The window's size100%Ctrl+= and Ctrl+-, or Settings, Accessibility, Zoom
Start here on the sampleShownHide at its foot

Lenses on the manuscript are never on until you pick one, and only one shows at a time. For every setting, see Settings.
