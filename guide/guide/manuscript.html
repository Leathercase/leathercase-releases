On this page

Manuscript ​

The manuscript view shows the whole story as one book, on one page that scrolls from the first line to the last. It is where you read your draft the way a reader meets it, see what the rest of Leathercase knows about any passage, leave comments, and jump back into your own writing app at the exact place you want to change. The page is read only. Leathercase never writes into your chapters: to change a sentence, you open it in the software you write in.

Opening the manuscript ​

Open a story, then pick Manuscript under the story's name in the sidebar. You can also press Ctrl+7 (Cmd+7 on macOS), pick Manuscript from the view menu in the breadcrumb, or type "Manuscript" in the command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K).

Other views send you here too, each to a precise place:

  • A scene picked on the plot grid, in the threads view or on a character's sheet opens the page scrolled to that scene's heading.
  • A lens offered from elsewhere opens with that lens on. The Open the accents lens button on a character's Voice tab is one (see Lenses and style checks).
  • Review beside a broken anchor on the Desk opens the page with the review of the first broken anchor already showing (see Broken anchors).
  • When a story's app has not been chosen yet and there are several that could open it, opening a file from elsewhere brings you to the manuscript first, so you can choose (see Open in).

What the page shows ​

The page is the story in reading order, with no file names or outline panel between the parts. Leathercase builds it from your files as they are:

On the pageWhere it comes from
Part and act headingsYour folders. Act I - The Dark Lamp becomes a heading above its chapters.
Chapter headingsEach note's name, with sort prefixes such as 01 taken off, so 01 The Night the Lamp Went Out.md reads The Night the Lamp Went Out.
Scene headingsThe headings inside a note (# The cottage, # The tower).
ParagraphsThe prose under them, with its emphasis shown as emphasis.
Scene breaksA horizontal rule (---) in the note shows as a thin line.

The order is the folder tree, the same order you see in your file manager: folders and notes sort by path, and numbers sort as numbers, so Chapter 2 comes before Chapter 10. A note can set its own place with story-order: in its front matter; notes with a number come first, by number (see Front matter).

Some notes never appear on the page:

  • Every file that is not declared as the story's prose. Only prose makes the book: notes about the story's world (characters, places, items, factions, events and kinds of your own), plans, records, work notes, research, scraps and files still unsorted all stay out (see How a story is read). In The Lamp at Ferrol, Characters/Nora Vidal.md and Places/Ferrol Point.md stay out.
  • A note with manuscript: false in its front matter. The Lamp at Ferrol's project note, The Lamp at Ferrol.md, carries it.
  • A note with no prose at all, such as an outline that is only a list.
  • Files your story's patterns leave out, such as a folder you untick in the story's file tree (see Story settings).

A story written in a single file reads as that file's headings in order.

How the book is cut into headings ​

Four preferences in Settings › Manuscript change how the notes become one book. They change only the page; the files are read as they are. They are kept on this machine, in preferences.json.

SettingWhat it doesDefault
Folder levels as headingsHow many folder levels become headings above the notes; 0 for none.2
Note names as headingsA note's name becomes a heading above its text.On
Strip from namesNumbers and separators drops sort prefixes such as 01 - from folder and note names; Nothing keeps names as they are.Numbers and separators
Reading speedWords a minute, for the reading time in the position bar (100 to 600).250

Inline emphasis ​

The page shows the marks of your text as a reader would see them, not as their symbols:

You wroteThe page shows
*italic* or _italic_italic
**bold** or __bold__bold
==highlight==highlighted text
a wiki link such as [[02 The Ledger]], or one with an alias after a vertical barthe alias when there is one, otherwise the note's name
[a link](https://…)the link's text alone
%% a comment %%nothing on the line; the comment is listed in the side column instead (see Comments and word lists)

Anything else stays as written. Quoted blocks (> …) show as quotations.

A screenplay on the page ​

A story written in Fountain, such as The Night Sleeper, shows as a script rather than as paragraphs. Sluglines are in capitals and action runs across the full width. Speech is indented as in a screenplay: the character's cue in capitals furthest in, a parenthetical under it, and the dialogue in a narrower column below. Transitions sit to the right, and centred text, lyrics, sections and synopses have their own styles. A Fountain title page shows its title at the top; a page break (===) shows as a line.

The lenses and the arrow keys do not read a script: they walk past it to the next prose note.

The position bar ​

The head of the view, where other views show a state line, holds a thin bar that fills as you scroll, and a line beside it:

34% · 1,712 of 5,040 words · 20 min

That is how far down the book you are, the words above the top of the page out of the whole, and the time it takes to read the whole book at your reading speed. When the story has comments, the line adds them: · 3 open of 5 comments.

The trail in the breadcrumb ​

As you scroll, the breadcrumb at the top of the window grows a trail after Manuscript: the part and the chapter at the top of the page, for example Act II - Salvage › The Reef › The boat. Each piece is a way back up: click it and the page scrolls to that heading. At the very top of the book the trail is empty. When the title bar is too narrow for the whole path, its middle folds behind … at its start.

The gutter ​

To the left of each paragraph, the gutter carries small marks:

  • The sentence meter. One tick per sentence, its width following the sentence's length in words. Sentences of ten words or more are a shade darker, and those of twenty-five or more darker still, so a paragraph's rhythm shows before you read it.
  • Comment dots. A dot when the paragraph carries one of your own comments from the note, and a dot in another colour when it carries an open comment of Leathercase's.
  • Thread marks. Where a directed thread plants something or pays it off on a quoted sentence, a mark just above that sentence shows ▲ for a plant and ▼ for a payoff or a reversal, with the thread's name and words such as Subplot: The ledger: plant, paid off in The vestry or Subplot: The lens: payoff, planted in The tower. A plant with no payoff yet says no payoff yet. Click the mark and the page jumps to the other end: from the plant to its payoff, or back.
  • Gauge marks. For a thread drawn with a scale, such as the sample's theme "What do we owe the drowned?", each scene where the thread moves ends with a mark carrying the thread's name, the word it reached and the running total, for example Theme: What do we owe the drowned?: doubt -1 at the end of The cottage. Hover it for the charge of that scene and whether the line crosses zero there.

Only stops anchored to a quoted sentence get a thread mark; a stop on a whole scene is too coarse a place for a mark. How to anchor stops is on the threads page, and below under Anchor a stop here.

Selecting a passage ​

Click a sentence and it is selected, with a soft highlight. Click the same sentence again and the selection clears. Drag across text to take in more than one sentence, even across paragraphs of the same note. Neither a click nor a drag leaves the app or touches the file.

The keys move the selection a paragraph at a time:

KeyDoes
clickselects a sentence
↑ ↓the previous or next paragraph (its first sentence)
Alt+↑ Alt+↓the previous or next chapter
Home Endthe first or last paragraph of the book
Enter or Ctrl/Cmd+Enteropens the passage in its app
Ctrl/Cmd+Jthe next broken anchor
Lthe next lens
Esccloses the review, then clears the selection, then turns the lens off

The footer names these keys; press ? for the full list in a small panel. Keys never fire while you type in a field.

The side column ​

The side column on the right tells you what the story knows about the selected passage. Toggle it with Ctrl/Cmd+B or the side column button at the right of the head. Its sections, top to bottom:

Passage ​

Before you pick anything it says: "Click a sentence to select it; drag to take in more. The column shows what the story knows about it, and opens it in your app."

With a passage selected it shows the passage in quotation marks (long passages cut at 160 characters), with the chapter and paragraph number as the section's hint, such as The Night the Lamp Went Out · ¶ 3. Under it:

ControlDoes
Open in and the app's name, such as Open in Obsidian (or Open in your default app)Opens the file in your writing app at the passage. Ctrl ↵ is printed on the button. See Open in.
Show file in folderOpens your file manager with the passage's file selected.
Copy passagePuts the selected text on the clipboard; the button reads Copied after.
Comment on this passageOpens the comment composer. See Comments and word lists.
Anchor a stop here…Opens the form that anchors a thread stop on the selected sentence. Shown only when one paragraph is selected, the passage is inside a scene the grid knows, and the story has a thread of your own.

The comments already on the selected paragraphs are listed below these, with Resolve and Remove for Leathercase's own.

Sentence log ​

The sentences of the selected paragraph that you rewrote or cut in earlier sittings, each struck through with the day and what replaced it, and Copy to take one back by hand. See Sentence log.

Threads here ​

Every thread that has a stop in the selected scene, one per line, with the stop's glyph, its role (plant, touch, payoff, reversal, or an arc's want, lie, turn, truth) and an audit mark. A thread whose stop is on this very passage shows brighter than the others. Hover a line for the stop's note. With none it says No thread stops in this scene.

The glyphs are the same everywhere in Leathercase:

GlyphMeans
hollow trianglea plant
filled trianglea payoff
turned trianglea reversal
dotany other stop
tick in a green ringverified: the stop's quoted sentence was found in the scene
! in a rose ringbroken: the quote no longer matches the scene
dashed ringplan: the stop has no quote yet

Scene ​

The scene's facts: POV, Time and Place, as the grid has them (not set or none found when they are missing). When characters are on stage it adds On stage: Nora Vidal, Bastian Kell., and for each character whose arc has no stop in the scene, a line such as Inés Roca’s arc has no stop in this scene.

Lens ​

The list of lenses, one at a time: No lens, Rhythm, Style checks, Dialogue, Echoes, Words, Accents. With a lens on and a paragraph selected, What the lens found lists every mark the lens made in that paragraph, with its note. Every lens is explained in Lenses and style checks. The footer at the bottom of the view also names the lens in force (lens: style checks); click it to step to the next.

All comments ​

Every comment of the book in one list, chapter by chapter. See Comments and word lists.

Open in your own app ​

The manuscript is read here and written elsewhere. Open in takes you from a passage straight to that place in the software you write in. Select a passage, then click the Open in button, press Enter, or press Ctrl/Cmd+Enter.

Leathercase goes as far into the file as the app allows:

App reachWhat happens
opens at lineThe app opens on the passage's paragraph.
opens at headingThe app opens at the scene heading above it.
opens fileThe app opens the file at its start.

Which app it uses is decided in this order: the app you chose for the story, then the app you chose for every story in that format on this machine, then an app Leathercase can infer, and last your system's default. When Leathercase inferred the app, a line under the button says why and where to change it, for example Inferred from the vault; choose another in Settings › Sources, Inferred from .scriv, or The only app here that opens .docx.

The first time you open a story whose app was never chosen, and several apps on this computer could open it, the column asks instead: Open this story in… lists the apps with how far each reaches, and the line "These apps on this computer open .md files. The choice is kept with the story." Pick one, leave Use for every story in .md ticked if you want the same app for all your Markdown stories, and click Open in with the app's name. The story's choice is kept in its settings; the choice for every story is kept in this machine's preferences. Change either later in Settings › Sources.

Open the whole story ​

Ctrl+Shift+O (Cmd+Shift+O on macOS) opens the story itself in your software, wherever you are in Leathercase. It is also Open the story in your software in the story's ⋯ menu in the sidebar, in the story's menu in the breadcrumb, and in the command palette. A file that is not a document, such as a folder, is shown in your file manager instead; Leathercase never runs a file.

Anchor a stop here ​

A thread stop can be anchored on the exact sentence where the thread moves, from the page. Select one sentence, click Anchor a stop here…, then fill the small form:

FieldWhat it is
ThreadOne of your own threads (the grid's POV and Time columns are not offered).
Roletouch, plant, payoff or reversal.
What the thread does hereA short note for the stop.

Click Anchor the stop. Leathercase writes the stop, with the selected sentence as its quote, to StoryThreads.md in your suite folder, never into your chapter. The status line at the foot of the page confirms it, such as Anchored The ledger at The tower, with Undo.

Broken anchors ​

An anchor is a quoted sentence that ties a thread stop to its place in the prose. When you rewrite that sentence in your own app, the quote no longer matches and the anchor is broken. The page helps you put it right.

Press Ctrl/Cmd+J. When nothing is broken, the status line says No broken anchors: every quoted stop is found in its scene. Otherwise a Review section opens at the top of the side column, with its count (1 of 3), and the page scrolls to the scene:

  • Anchor no longer matches, then the thread, the role and the scene, such as The ledger · plant · The tower.
  • Was: the old quote.
  • Closest now: the sentence of the scene that shares the most words with it, highlighted on the page. When none shares a word: No sentence of the scene shares its words.
  • A line on how much changed: Most of the quote's words are still there: this looks like an edit to the same moment., or Much of the wording changed. Check the stop still holds before anchoring it again., or, when the quote is gone, The quote is gone from the scene. Keep the stop as a plan until the moment is written again, or remove it.

Three buttons settle it:

ButtonDoes
Anchor to the new passageRe-anchors the stop on the closest sentence now.
Keep as a plan, no anchorKeeps the stop, without a quote.
Remove the stopTakes the stop out of the thread.

Each writes StoryThreads.md only. Ctrl/Cmd+J again goes to the next broken anchor; Esc closes the review. The review closes by itself when the last one is dealt with.

Undo and redo ​

Everything the page writes (a comment made, resolved, reopened or removed, a stop anchored, re-anchored, kept as a plan or removed) goes on one undo stack. The status line at the foot of the page names the last step, such as Commented on ¶ 3 of The Night the Lamp Went Out, with Undo and the number of steps in the stack; Redo appears after an undo. Before anything is done it reads Comments go to Comments.md in your suite folder; your notes are never written.

An undo writes the file back only if it still holds what Leathercase wrote. If the file changed outside Leathercase in the meantime, nothing is written and the status line says The file changed outside Leathercase since; nothing was written, and the step was dropped.

When the page is empty ​

A story with no prose yet shows: "No chapter yet. The manuscript is every note that is not a character, a place or a thing, in the order of the folder tree; write one and it appears here." Since file roles, that means every file declared as the story's prose: a chapter written where nothing claims it waits, unsorted, on How this story is read until you say it is prose. Write a chapter in your app; Leathercase watches the story's folder while the story is open, so the page shows the chapter soon after you save it.

What Leathercase writes ​

The manuscript page writes only Leathercase's own files, in the story's suite folder:

FileWhen
Comments.mdA comment made, resolved, reopened or removed on the page.
StoryThreads.mdA stop anchored, re-anchored, kept as a plan or removed.
WordLists.mdA word put in or taken out of a word list from the words lens.
Accents.mdA word given to a character from the accents lens.

Your chapters are only ever read.
