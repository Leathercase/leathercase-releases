Find, open and make ​

Leathercase has one field for finding things and making them: the find field in the middle of the title bar. Type a few letters and it lists the stories, views, commands, cards, tags, notes and, inside a story, its people, places and scenes. Type a word and a colon, character: Inés Roca, and it makes one. This page covers that field, the search inside a view, the search of your stories' text, and the capture window that takes a line from any app.

Open the field ​

There are three ways in, and they all put the caret in the same field:

Do thisWhat happens
Click the field in the title barThe list drops under it
Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on macOS)Opens the field, or closes it if it is open. The key is shown in the field while it is closed
Ctrl+OFind or make in this view: opens the field from any view

The field reads Find anything, or make it: character: Nora until you type. While it is open:

  • ↑ and ↓ move through the list.
  • Enter runs the row in hand.
  • Esc, or a click anywhere else, closes the field. Esc closes only the field; it does not take you back a screen.

The line at the foot of the list repeats these keys: ↑ ↓ move, enter run, esc close and word: title makes it.

What the empty field shows ​

With nothing typed, the list is a map of the app:

  • Recent: the commands you ran last.
  • Stories: every story of the home, each with a word on where it stands (on pace, behind, no target, not read yet and so on). Pick one to open it.
  • Go, Story, View and App: every command the app has, grouped, each with its key. Go holds the places and views (Library, Desk, Writer, Tags, Plot grid, Graph, Manuscript and the rest); Story the commands on the open story, such as Export the manuscript as one file and Story settings; View the commands on the view on screen; App those on the app itself, such as Settings and the themes.

A command that needs a story only shows when a story is open.

Under the list, the mate may add a line headed Not tried yet: one command you have not used, with Try it and Don't suggest this. On the sample home, and once you have hidden it, Start here is offered here too. Both work on this computer with no model.

Find something ​

Type and the list narrows as you go. Matching ignores case and accents, so ines finds Inés. Each section shows its first six matches:

SectionWhat it listsWhat picking it does
StoriesStories whose name matchesOpens the story
In this storyThe open story's characters, places, items, factions and events, with their kindOpens the Graph with that one pinned
ScenesThe open story's scenes by title, with the note they are inOpens the Manuscript at that scene: its heading at the top of the page and its first sentence selected
CardsThe Writer Board's cards, with their kind, or card or your noteOpens the Writer Board with the card selected
TagsEvery tag of the home, as #tagOpens the Tags view with the tag selected
NotesEvery note of the home by its name, with the folder it sits in. A long folder is cut short and the name kept; rest the pointer on it to read it whole. The daily snapshots in suite/daily/ are not listedOpens the note in the program you write in. A card Leathercase made opens on the board instead
Go, Story, View, AppCommands whose name matchesRuns the command

In this story and Scenes appear only while a story is open. A name Leathercase found in the prose with no note of its own (a candidate) is not listed here; find it in the Graph.

When nothing matches, the list says Nothing matches. Esc closes.

Make something with a word and a colon ​

A word, a colon and a title makes a thing. As soon as you type the first letters of a word, the section Make with a word offers the words that fit, each with what it makes: type cha and it offers character:. Picking a word puts it in the field, ready for the title.

Once there is a title after the colon, the first row of the list is Make, saying what it will make and where. Press Enter to make it.

A command whose name starts with a word and a colon is still found by its name. Type Theme: light in full and the command Theme: light comes first, above the Make row, so Enter runs it. Type less, theme: li, and Make comes first.

The kinds come from the Writer Board's framework, so a kind you add there (see Kinds) becomes a word here. The words themselves are the field's grammar: they stay in English whatever language the app is in.

Where a made thing opens ​

What you make opens where it belongs, with the caret in its text so you can write it straight away:

  • A card opens on the Writer Board, selected, its text in hand in the side column. It is a markdown note in the home's Writer/ folder, tagged for its list. See Cards are notes.
  • A character, place, event or note for the story opens in the Graph, pinned, with its note open to write in. It is a note in the story's suite/ folder: suite/Characters/Inés Roca.md, suite/Places/…, suite/Events/… or suite/Notes/…, typed in its front matter. If the story already has a note of yours for the same name, the Graph shows yours and Leathercase's note sits beside it as its companion. A title already taken gets a number, so no note is ever overwritten.
  • A tag goes on the card in hand when you are on the Writer Board with one of Leathercase's cards selected. Otherwise it is entered in the home's suite/Tags.md and the Tags view opens with it selected.
  • A mark is written into the story's sentence log. Mark the sentence log in the Story commands puts mark: in the field for you. While you name a mark, the list shows only the mark's row: nothing else is found.

Nothing you make here is written into your own notes.

Plain text makes an idea ​

Text with no word and colon finds, and also offers to make: the last row of the list is Make, New card with your text as its title, in Ideas, an idea. Enter on it makes the card and opens it on the board. The row is not offered when a card already has that title, so pressing Enter twice does not make two.

A question is never made into a card. Text ending in ?, or starting with "how do I", "how to" or "how can I", gets the mate's answer instead (see below).

Ctrl+F: search in this view ​

Ctrl+F (Search in this view) goes to the search field of the view on screen, when the view has one:

ViewIts field
GraphFind a name, in the side column. If the column is folded, Ctrl+F opens it
CharactersFilter the cast
Any other viewCtrl+F opens the find field in the title bar

Search your stories' text ​

Type three letters or more, with no colon and not a question, and the find field also searches the text of every story in the home. The passages appear under the list:

  • Words: passages that hold the words you typed, with the words marked, each with its story and scene and a words label. If none do, it says No passage has these words.
  • Pick a passage and the story opens on it: the manuscript at that scene.

Words match whole, so went never finds twenty. The last word is the one you are still typing, so it also matches by its beginning: the lamp w finds "the lamp went out". A long word allows a slip of a letter. Passages that hold your words as you typed them, in that order, come first; passages that hold them apart follow.

The search reads what you wrote: the scenes of your stories and the sentences in their logs. It leaves out Leathercase's own files, the daily snapshots, and comments hidden in your notes, which are also taken out of the passage it shows.

While the search runs, the field says Searching the stories…. Word search is plain matching, done on this computer, and needs nothing installed.

Search by meaning ​

Meaning search finds passages that say the same thing in other words, in any language your stories use: a search for "the light failed" may find a passage where the lamp went out. It needs a small model, downloaded once; after that it runs on this computer, and your text never leaves it.

Leathercase asks about it once, the first time a word search finds nothing. The box Search by meaning, not on this computer yet, gives the model's name, licence and size, with:

  • Download and the size, to fetch it. A bar shows Downloading the meaning model, with Pause, Resume and Cancel. Then Then it reads your stories once, with a count of stories. Word search works meanwhile.
  • Not now, which keeps word search and does not ask again for a week.

If a download does not finish, the box says why and offers Try again.

Once meaning search is here, a switch above the results chooses Words and meaning, Words or Meaning. Meaning results come under Meaning, closest first, each labelled meaning · close or meaning · related, and the foot of the list says meaning runs on this computer.

Ask how to do something ​

Type a question, such as how do I export?, and under the list the mate answers under How do I: the steps, from the app's commands, this guide or its skills, and a button Run: with the command, and its key. When nothing on this computer answers, it says Nothing on this computer answers that. and offers the Nearest command. No model is used.

Capture a line from any app ​

The capture window takes a line from wherever you are, a quote in a browser, a thought in another program, and makes it a card in the Writer's Ideas list, where it waits for a story.

Turn on the key ​

The capture key works anywhere on the computer, so it is off until you turn it on. In Settings, open Hotkeys. Under Anywhere on this computer:

  • Turn on global keys turns on both keys that work outside Leathercase.
  • Capture to Writer is Ctrl+Shift+N by default: it opens a small window over any app; what you type lands in the Writer's Ideas.
  • Show Leathercase is Ctrl+Shift+S by default: it brings Leathercase forward from any app.

Click a key to change it, then press the keys you want; changing a key turns global keys on. If another app holds the key, Settings says so in amber and pressing it does nothing; pick other keys. Turn off global keys lets both go, so other apps can have them. These keys belong to this computer and live in its preferences, not in your writing home.

Use the window ​

Press the capture key in any app. A small window titled Capture to Writer opens over it, with the caret in its one field. Its top right corner names the writing home it will land in, followed by Ideas (the last home you were in).

  1. Type or paste. The placeholder says it: A line, a quote, a what-if. The first line becomes the card's title.
  2. If the home has stories, To chooses where it goes: Ideas, in the Writer (the default), or Loose on a story's beat board. The window remembers your last choice.
  3. Press Ctrl+Enter or Save. The footer says Saved and the file it wrote, and the window closes.

Esc or Cancel closes it without saving. Before you type, the footer says The card lands in Ideas, where it waits for a board.; as you type, it shows the file the card will be.

The title is the first line of what you typed, with the quotation marks around it taken off and cut at a word near sixty characters. The whole text is the card. Captured to Ideas, it is a card note in the home's Writer/ folder, tagged writer/idea and of the kind idea. Captured to a beat board, it is a loose card note in that story's suite/Beats/ folder; see Beat board. When you come back to Leathercase, the card is on the board.

If no writing home is open yet, the window says Open a writing home in Leathercase first.
