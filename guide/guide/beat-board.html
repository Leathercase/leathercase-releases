Beat board ​

The beat board is where a story is first explored, before there is a grid to fill. It lays out the beats of a structure (Three acts, Save the Cat, the Hero's journey, Kishōtenketsu, or a template of your own) as columns grouped by act, and you put cards in them freely. A card is a short markdown note Leathercase keeps for you; nothing on the board is prose, and the board never opens your manuscript files. When a card is ready, you can send it on to the plot grid as a planned scene.

Opening the board ​

Open a story, then pick Beat board under the story's name in the sidebar. It is the first of the story's views. You can also press Ctrl+9 (Cmd+9 on macOS), or type "Beat board" in the command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K).

While the cards are read from disk the board says Reading the beat board…. The board reads its cards again every time the Leathercase window comes back to the front, so a card you captured over another app, or a card note you changed in your own editor, shows as soon as you return.

What the board shows ​

The acts run left to right; the loose cards wait in a tray below them:

PartWhat it is
An actA heading with the act's name (Act I, Act II, Act III for Three acts; Ki: introduction and so on for Kishōtenketsu), and under it a column for each of its beats.
A columnThe beat's name, the number of cards in it, and a line under the head saying what happens at that beat, taken from the template (for Midpoint: "A false win or a false loss that changes what the hero wants"). A column you added yourself is marked yours and has no such line.
A cardIts title, the first lines of its text (point at them to read it whole), its tags as coloured chips, and small marks when they apply: beat: Crisis on a loose card that keeps a beat the template lacks, and on the grid on a card already sent to the grid.
Not placedThe tray of loose cards below the columns, as wide as the pane, its cards wrapping onto as many lines as they need. It scrolls down with the board, so it never covers a tall column, and it stays as wide as the pane however far the acts scroll sideways. Its line reads "Cards with no beat yet, and those whose beat this template does not have." Click its head to fold it away; folded, the head still counts its cards (Not placed 6), and a click opens it again.

With the loose cards below, the columns read left to right, top to bottom, as the story alone: the order of its turns and revelations. A change to a template you never laid out puts every card in the tray, which fills downward instead of widening the board.

The line at the top of the view is the board's state: how many cards it holds, how many are loose, and the template in force, for example 4 cards · 1 loose · Three acts. On an empty board it reads no cards yet · none loose · Three acts. Under it runs the board's tags with their counts; see Tags on the board.

The footer names the keys the board answers to; press ? to see them in a small panel and Esc to put it away. The side column on the right holds what you can do with the card or column in hand, and the About section. Toggle it with the side column button at the right of the head, or Ctrl/Cmd+B.

Tag chips take the colour you gave the tag in the Tags view.

Tags on the board ​

Once a card carries a tag, a row headed Tags sits under the state line. It shows every tag on the board's cards with the number of cards that carry it, most used first, for example #theme/truth 1 and #lamp 1. Two spellings of one tag (#Lamp and #lamp) count as one.

Point at a tag, or reach it with Tab, and a small box under it says where its cards sit, column by column in the board's order, the loose ones last: Inciting incident 1, Climax 1. Columns without the tag are left out.

Click a tag and every card without it is dimmed, loose cards included, so you can see where that thread runs across the beats: read left to right and top to bottom, it is the order the story reveals it in. The tag stays pressed and Show all appears at the end of the row. Click the tag again, or Show all, to bring every card back. Clicking another tag moves the light to it. If the last card carrying the tag loses it, the board lets go of the tag by itself.

The counts are for the whole board, not for one column. Nothing is written when you pick a tag.

Making a card ​

Every column, Loose included, has + a card at its foot. Click it, or press N on the board: with a card picked, N opens the form in that card's column; with a column picked, in that column; otherwise in Loose.

The form is one field, typed as markdown:

  • The first line is the card's title. It may start with # as a heading or not.
  • Enter on a one-line draft makes the card at once.
  • Shift+Enter starts a new line; everything after the first line is the card's text. With more than one line, Ctrl/Cmd+Enter makes the card.
  • A #tag anywhere in the title or the text becomes a tag. It leaves the text and shows as a chip. #place/coast works too.
  • Esc or Cancel closes the form without making anything.

The button reads Add until there is a title, then Add "…" with the start of it. The line under the form repeats the keys: "enter adds · shift+enter for the text · a #tag becomes a tag".

The card lands at the foot of the column. If a card of the same title already exists, the new one gets a number (Storm 2), so no card is ever written over.

Where cards live ​

Each card is a markdown note in the story's author data folder, under Beats/, named after its title. For The Lamp at Ferrol that is The Lamp at Ferrol/suite/Beats/. The About section of the side column prints the exact folder. A card made in Midpoint with the tag mystery reads:

markdown
---
beat: Midpoint
created: 2026-10-06
tags: [mystery]
---
Nora finds the lamp lit when it should be dark.

The note says everything without Leathercase: its beat, its tags, the day it was made, and its text as the body. A card with no beat: line is loose. Characters a file name cannot hold (/ \ : * ? " < > | # ^ [ ]) become spaces in the file name.

The board keeps one file of its own, BeatBoard.json in the story's suite/ folder. It records the template in force and a layout for every template you have laid the cards out on: the columns you added to it, the order of the cards in each column and in Loose, and, for the templates not in force, which column each card sat in. Delete it and the cards stay where their notes say for the template in force; your columns, the order and the other templates' layouts are lost.

A beat: line you change by hand in your own editor wins for the template in force: the card shows in that column the next time the board reads its cards. The other templates' layouts are not changed by it.

See Files Leathercase writes for every file in the story's suite/ folder.

Moving a card ​

Drag a card onto another column and drop it where you want it among the cards there. The card's note has its beat: line rewritten to the new column's name; nothing else in it changes. Drop it on Loose and the line is taken out.

Dragging within a column reorders it. The order is kept in BeatBoard.json, not in the notes. A card that is not in the kept order yet (one you made in your own editor, say) comes after the ordered ones, oldest first.

You can also move a card from the side column with its Beat list; see below.

Reordering a card within its own column leaves its note alone: the beat: line stays as written, whatever its spelling or language, and only the order in BeatBoard.json changes. The board never rewrites a beat it did not move. The same holds for a loose card that keeps a beat the current template lacks.

Picking a card ​

Click a card to pick it, or walk the cards with the left and right arrow keys, column by column, then the loose cards in the tray. Esc, or a click on the board's background, puts the selection away. The side column then shows a Selected section headed with the card's title, the path of its note, and these controls:

ControlWhat it does
The text fieldThe card's text. Press Enter on the board to put the caret here. It is saved when you leave the field or press Ctrl/Cmd+Enter, and saved too if you switch view while typing. A #tag typed here becomes a tag.
TagsThe card's tags as chips; the × on a chip takes that tag off. The field below ("a tag, new or known") suggests tags already used on the board; + a tag puts the typed one on. A leading # is dropped. With no tags the row says none.
BeatEvery column of the board, with loose first. Choosing one moves the card to the foot of that column; "moving the card rewrites the beat line in its note". For a loose card that keeps a beat the template lacks, the first choice reads "Crisis, not on this template".
Put on the plot gridSends the card to the plot grid as a planned scene. See From card to scene.
Delete the cardDeletes the card. See Deleting a card.
closePuts the selection away.

Editing a card in place ​

Double-click a card to edit it on the board itself. The card becomes one field, as when it was made: the first line is its title, the rest its text.

  • Ctrl/Cmd+Enter or Save saves it. Clicking anywhere outside the card saves it too.
  • Esc or Cancel puts the card back as it was.
  • A new first line renames the card: its note is renamed to the new title and keeps its place in the column. A title another card has is refused, and the card is not saved until it changes. A title that only changes case keeps the note's name.
  • A #tag typed in the title or the text becomes a tag, as when the card was made. The tags the card already has stay.

The strip says "Renamed Storm to Gale" for a new title, or "Wrote in Storm" for a new text, and Undo puts the note back whole.

Your own columns ​

The template's beats always stay on the board. Between them you can put columns of your own, an "Aftermath" after the climax, say.

  • To add one after a column, click that column's head. The side column shows the column; type a name in the field ("a column of your own") and click + a column after.
  • To add one before every beat, use the field in the About section and click + a column first.

A new column sits in the act of the column it follows. With Aftermath added after Climax, Act III reads Climax, Aftermath, Falling action, Resolution. Two columns cannot share a name; the board refuses the second.

Click the head of one of your columns and the side column says a column of yours, with two more controls:

  • Rename: type the new name and click Rename. Every card in the column has its beat: line rewritten to the new name.
  • Take the column off: the column goes, and "its cards become loose and their notes lose the beat". Columns that followed it now follow what it followed.

Your columns belong to the template in force. Another template has its own, which you add there in the same way; switch back and yours are where you left them.

Click the head of a template's beat and the side column says, for example, "a beat of Three acts; it stays on the board", with the act's name and the beat's line. It can be the place you add a column after, and it can be sent to the plot grid whole, but it cannot be renamed or taken off.

Choosing a template ​

Open the board's ⋯ menu (Beat board menu). Under the heading Template it lists every template that has beats, the one in force in the accent colour:

TemplateActs and beats
Three acts (the default)Act I: Opening, Inciting incident, First turn. Act II: Rising action, Midpoint, Crisis. Act III: Climax, Falling action, Resolution.
Save the CatAct I: Opening image, Theme stated, Set-up, Catalyst, Debate, Break into two. Act II: B story, Fun and games, Midpoint, Bad guys close in, All is lost, Dark night of the soul, Break into three. Act III: Finale, Final image.
Hero's journeyDeparture: Ordinary world, Call to adventure, Refusal of the call, Meeting the mentor, Crossing the threshold. Initiation: Tests, allies, enemies; Approach to the inmost cave; Ordeal; Reward. Return: The road back, Resurrection, Return with the elixir.
KishōtenketsuFour acts, Ki: introduction, Shō: development, Ten: twist and Ketsu: reconciliation, with one beat each: Introduction, Development, Twist, Reconciliation.
Your ownAny template you saved from the grid that has beats. See Plot grid, templates.

The two built-in templates without beats, An arc per character and Story analysis, are grid templates only and do not appear here.

What changing the template does ​

Each template is its own arrangement of the same cards. You read the board left to right and top to bottom as the order of the story, and that order is kept for each template apart.

When you pickWhat happens
A template you have never laid outEvery card starts in Loose, even a card whose beat has the same name in both templates (both Three acts and Save the Cat have a Midpoint). They come in the order you read the board you left: its columns left to right, each top to bottom, then its loose cards. You then lay the cards out on it as you like.
A template you laid out beforeEvery card goes back to the column it had there, in the order it had, with the columns you added to that template. Cards made since wait in Loose, after its own loose cards, in the order you read the board you left.
The template in forceNothing changes.

The cards slide from where they stood to their new places, so you can follow each one. If your system asks for less motion, they move without the slide.

The board you leave is kept as it stands: switch back to it and everything is where you left it.

Each card's beat: line is rewritten to its column on the new template, since the note says where the card sits on the board in force. A card that lands in Loose loses its line only when the line would put it in one of the new template's columns. A beat the new template has no column for stays as written, and the card shows it as a mark (beat: Crisis). So a card that is loose under both templates is never touched.

One Undo takes the whole change back: the template, every note it rewrote and the layouts.

The story's language ​

A template's beats are words in the story's language, set in Story settings. In a story declared in Português (Brasil), Three acts reads Ato I: Abertura, Incidente incitante, Primeira virada, and a card made there says beat: Abertura. An open board follows when you change the story's language.

A template's layouts are kept by the position of each beat in the template, not by its name, so a change of the story's language leaves the board as it was: the cards stay in their columns and in their order, now under the beats' names in the new language.

A card whose beat: line names a beat of a built-in template in another of the app's languages (English, Português (Brasil), Português (Portugal)) still sits in that beat's column; a card that says beat: Midpoint sits in Ponto médio. Its line is left as written until you move the card to another column, which writes the beat in the story's language; reordering it within its own column leaves the line alone. A beat that is in none of those languages, or that belongs to a template of your own, is matched by name only; a card whose beat matches no column waits in Loose, marked with it.

Snapshots ​

A snapshot keeps the board as it stands, to set beside a later one: how the shape of the story moved while you explored it.

Open the board's ⋯ menu and choose Keep a snapshot. The strip says "Snapshot kept: 19 Oct, 16:19". Each click keeps a new one, so you can keep several in a day. If nothing on the board changed since the last snapshot, nothing is kept and the strip says "The board is as it was in the snapshot of 19 Oct, 16:19; nothing kept".

A snapshot holds the template in force, the acts and columns in their order, each column's cards in their order with their titles, text and tags, the loose cards in their order, and the tag counts.

Once one exists, a row of tabs sits over the board: Now, then one tab per snapshot, newest first, named by its day and time ("19 Oct, 16:19"); two kept in the same minute are told apart by the seconds ("19 Oct, 16:19:25"). A snapshot tab draws the board as it stood, read only: the state line names the moment and ends in read only. Its Tags row counts that snapshot's tags, and a tag picked there dims its cards too. Nothing on a snapshot can be moved or edited, and the board's keys do nothing there; click Now to go back.

The Snapshots section of the side column lists them, newest first, each with its card count and its template. Click one to open its tab, again to go back to Now. With none it says "None yet. A snapshot keeps the board's order, its loose cards and its tag counts as machine data, to set beside a later one."

To delete a snapshot, click Delete beside it in the section. The button turns into Click again to delete; a second click deletes it, and Keep disarms it. A deleted snapshot cannot be brought back with undo. Deleting the one on view takes you back to Now.

Snapshots are machine data, kept in the story's suite/snapshots/ folder as BeatBoard 2026-10-19 16-19-25.json, next to the grid's. They are never read back into the board.

Setting two moments side by side ​

On a snapshot's tab, the end of the tab row has Compare with. Choose the board now, or another snapshot, and the board is drawn as the later of the two, read only, with every card marked by what happened to it since the earlier:

MarkMeans
newThe card was not on the earlier board.
goneThe card was on the earlier board and is not on the later one. It is drawn faint and struck through, where it stood. One whose column the later board does not have waits in the tray, marked gone from Crisis.
from CrisisThe card stood in another column then, or in Loose (from Loose).
reorderedThe card stayed in its column but changed its place among the cards there. Only the cards that broke the order are marked: move one card from the top to the bottom and only that card is.
rewrittenThe card's text or tags changed.

The state line says the two moments and the count of each mark, for example From 19 Oct, 16:19 to now · 1 new · 1 gone · 1 moved · 0 reordered · 0 rewritten · read only. In the Tags row each count is the later one, followed by its change since the earlier (#lamp 3 +1); a tag no card carries any more shows 0 and its loss.

Cards are known by their title, which is their note's name, so a card renamed between the two moments shows as one gone and one new. Columns are matched by name, so two boards on different templates are still compared card by card; a line over the board then says "The template changed from Three acts to Kishōtenketsu: cards are compared by title, and a card in a column of another name counts as moved."

Choose nothing to see the snapshot alone again. Going to another tab, or deleting the snapshot set beside, ends the comparison.

Deleting a card ​

Two ways:

  • Pick the card and press Delete (or Backspace). It goes at once, and the strip offers Undo.
  • In the side column click Delete the card. The button turns into Click again to delete; a second click deletes, and Keep disarms it.

The note goes to the system's trash, not straight off the disk, and Ctrl/Cmd+Z brings it back.

Undo and the strip ​

Under the board runs a strip. Before anything is written it says "Pick a card and press Enter to write in it." After each change it names what was written, such as Moved Storm to Climax, Made the card Lamp, Put #grief on Storm, Wrote in Storm, Added the column Aftermath or Laid the columns out from Kishōtenketsu, with Undo beside it and how many steps are in the stack. Redo appears once something has been undone.

Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes and Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z redoes, whenever the caret is not in a text field (inside a field they are the field's own). Undo puts back exactly what the change wrote: the card notes, BeatBoard.json, and the grid file when a card was sent to it. If a file was changed outside Leathercase since, undo writes nothing and the strip says "The file changed outside Leathercase since; nothing was written, and the step was dropped." Your edit is never wiped.

The stack lives as long as the board is open. Keeping or deleting a snapshot is not a step in it.

From card to scene ​

When a card is ready to be a scene, pick it and click Put on the plot grid. The card becomes a planned scene in the story's grid file, PlotGrid.md in the story's suite/ folder:

  • It goes under its beat's act, after the last scene that carries the same beat. If no scene carries it yet, it goes at the end of the act, and the act is made if the grid lacks it. Acts are matched by name: in the sample, whose acts are called Act I - The Dark Lamp and so on, a card from Climax lands under a new act named Act III, in a group headed No chapter yet.
  • The card's title becomes the scene's title, its text the scene's summary, and the scene carries the beat.
  • The card stays on the board, marked on the grid, and its note gains a line outline: "[[PlotGrid#Storm]]". The side column then reads "on the plot grid as [[PlotGrid#Storm]]; putting it there again writes nothing".

From then on the scene is yours to work on in the plot grid, where it shows as a planned row. The card and the scene are not kept in step: editing one does not change the other.

In a story written in Leathercase, Write the scene sits beside Put on the plot grid. It puts the card on the grid if it is not there yet, then writes its scene into the story's Manuscript/ folder, as the grid's own Write the scene does, and the strip says "The scene is written into the Manuscript folder". A scene already written is not written again.

A loose card cannot be sent; the button is greyed and says "A loose card has no beat to go under; put it in a column first." A card sent twice is written once, and the strip says "Storm is already on the plot grid; nothing was written".

To send a whole column, click its head and then Send the column to the plot grid. Each card becomes a planned scene in the column's order; "a card already there is left as it is", and the strip counts how many were. On an empty column the button is greyed and says "no cards to send". For a column of your own, its scenes go after the scenes of the columns before it, back to the template's beat it follows.

Undo takes the scene out of the grid file and the outline: line out of the card.

Capturing a card from anywhere ​

The capture window (see The Writer Board) can send a line straight to a story's beat board. In its To list choose "Loose on The Lamp at Ferrol's beat board"; the card lands in Loose, and the next capture starts on the same board.

Proposals from agents ​

An agent you connected with Lay out can propose new cards, moves and the board's template. They show in the proposal style beside your own cards while the assistant's layer is on (Ctrl/Cmd+.). Nothing is written until you accept. An agent can also read the snapshots you kept, and set one beside another or beside the board now, as the comparison above does; it cannot keep or delete one. Review in Beat board, on a changeset in the inbox, opens the board with a review bar beside it, so you judge each proposal where it would land. See Proposals and the inbox.
