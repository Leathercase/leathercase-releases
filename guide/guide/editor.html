Editor ​

The editor is where you write a story in Leathercase. It opens one story in a window of its own, as one long page of scenes, and saves each scene to a plain markdown file as you type. Nothing it writes is hidden from you: the files on disk are exactly what you typed.

You write the prose. No agent and no model ever writes, moves or renames a file of it, in the editor or anywhere else in Leathercase.

Opening the editor ​

Open a story, then pick Editor at the foot of the story's views in the sidebar. You can also:

  • press Ctrl+Shift+E (Cmd+Shift+E on macOS);
  • pick Write the story in the editor from the story's menu (the ⋯ beside its name), or from the Story menu;
  • type "editor" in the command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K).

Each story has one editor window. Opening it again brings the window you already have to the front. The main window stays where it was, so you can keep the plot grid or the Graph beside the text.

The Manuscript folder ​

A story you write in Leathercase keeps its prose in a folder inside the story's own folder, named Manuscript. The folder is the structure:

In Manuscript/Is
A folderAn act
A folder inside an actA chapter
A markdown fileA scene
A # heading inside a sceneA section of that scene

Order comes from the numbers at the start of the names, as 01 The Ledger.md and 02 The Quay.md, and numbers sort as numbers. Leathercase gives and keeps these numbers as you add scenes, chapters and acts; you never type them.

Your story's other notes (characters, places, research) stay where they are and are read as before. Only the prose lives in Manuscript/.

Importing a story ​

A story read from Word, Scrivener, Fountain or a folder of your own notes is read, never written. The first time you open the editor on such a story, it says so and offers Import into the Manuscript folder.

Importing copies the story's scenes as markdown into Manuscript/, keeping their folders, names, headings and order. A Word file becomes a markdown file; a Scrivener document comes out of its project. Your originals stay as they are: nothing is moved, changed or deleted. From then on the story is read from its Manuscript/ copy alone, so nothing is counted twice, and the originals are no longer read.

Comments written as %% … %% in the originals, on one line or as a block over several, move out of the copies as they do when you type one: a short name is who speaks the line after it, anything else a comment beside the sentence before it, in the story's Comments.md. The originals keep theirs.

A story reads one source at a time. Importing again never writes over a scene you already have in Manuscript/.

The window ​

The page holds every scene of the story, one after another, each under its title. Type anywhere. Markdown shows as you mean it, as Obsidian's live preview does: *italics* read as italics, and the marks come back while the cursor is on them.

A scene's front matter (the --- block at the top of its file) is folded behind one faint ··· properties line. Click it to show the properties; the cursor stays out of them while they are folded.

The title bar says where you are (Manuscript › The Quay) and whether the scene is saved, saving or unsaved. The bar under it says how much you wrote: scene 3 of 24 · 1,208 words · 312 words this sitting.

Saving ​

Each scene is saved to its own file a moment after you stop typing. There is no Save button.

If a scene's file changes in another app while it is open here, Leathercase writes nothing over it and says: "This scene changed in another app since you opened it. Nothing was written over." Keep mine writes your version; Take the other loads the file as it now is.

Closing the window, or quitting Leathercase, waits until every scene is saved. See Keeping your work safe.

Zen and typewriter ​

Two switches sit at the right of the bar under the title bar, beside the ⋯ menu:

  • Zen (Ctrl+., Cmd+. on macOS) shows the text and the novel's name, nothing else. Move the pointer to the top right corner for its switches: zen, typewriter and full screen. Esc or Ctrl/Cmd+. leaves it.
  • Typewriter keeps the line you are writing on a quiet band across the middle of the window. The wheel moves the text behind it.

What shows beside the text ​

The editor opens with as little as it can: the text, the Contents and the document map. The ⋯ menu turns each aid on or off, and Leathercase remembers your choice on this computer. A comment or a speaker you add turns its aid on, so what you just wrote never disappears.

AidWhat it showsAt first
Names and aliasesThe story's names, and their other names, coloured by kind as you write them. Point at one for a card; Open beside the text shows its noteOff
Who speaksWho says or thinks each line of dialogueOff
Comments beside the textYour comments, in a lane at the right, each beside its passageOff
Document mapThe whole story in miniature down the right edge; click to go thereOn
ContentsThe acts, chapters and scenes, at the left of the text; click to go to oneOn
Story notes panelThe story's characters, places, items, factions, events and other notes, at the left, to open beside the textOff
Full screenThe window fills the screenOff

A scene's title folds it: its text hides and its title says how many words it holds.

Commands ​

Type / at the start of a line or after a space to open the command box. Keep typing to narrow it, ↑ and ↓ to choose, Enter to run, Esc to close. A command never stays in your text: running it takes the typed command out.

The box groups them:

Structure ​

CommandWhat it does
/sceneA new scene here. The text after the cursor goes into it. A heading right after the cursor names it; otherwise it is "Untitled"
/chapterA new chapter after this one, with the text after the cursor
/actA new act after this one, with the text after the cursor
/undoPuts back the last /scene, /chapter, /act or rename
/pov NoraSets the scene's point of view

Double-click a scene's, chapter's or act's title, over the text or in the Contents, to rename it. Its number stays, and Leathercase's own files follow the new name: their links to the scene, its comments, and who speaks in it.

/chapter says so when the scenes sit directly in an act, and /act when the story has no acts.

After a new scene, chapter, act or rename, an Undo button shows beside the counts for a few seconds; /undo works for as long as the window is open, the last twenty back. An undo puts every file the command touched as it was. If you wrote in one of those scenes since, it does nothing and says so, since putting the file back would lose what you wrote; the text from before the command is in the story's history either way.

Type the word after the command, or run the command right after a word to look that word up. The box shows the words grouped, each with a note, and your sentence with the chosen word in place, so you see whether it fits. Enter puts it where the command was typed.

What leaves this computer ​

The editor goes online for one thing only: the words commands, when you run one, and only if Ask Datamuse is on in Settings › Words.

WhoWhat it receivesWhen
Datamuse, onlineThe word you look up and the words beside it, nothing moreWhen you run /th, /rhyme, /means, /related, /describe or /sounds
Your local model, through OllamaThe passage around the wordWhen you run a words command, if you chose a model

The local model runs on this computer, and nothing it reads leaves it. No model in the cloud is asked for words. Answers are kept in words-cache.json in the app's data, so the same word is instant next time.

Datamuse answers in English only. /tc needs a local model; without one it says where to choose one.

Working offline ​

Everything in the editor works offline but the words commands. For those:

  • With the internet, Datamuse answers.
  • Without it, a local model answers, if you have one: install Ollama, then choose or download a model in Settings › Words.
  • With neither, the box says so and points to Settings › Words.

Writing scenes from the plot grid and the beat board ​

A scene planned on the plot grid can become a file. In a story written in Leathercase, a planned row's menu has Write the scene, and a chapter's band has Write the planned scenes. Each is written into Manuscript/ at the grid's levels, as a heading in its chapter's file, after the scene before it, and the row is linked to it.

On the beat board, a card's side column has Write the scene too: the card goes to the plot grid first if it is not there, then its row is written the same way.

Keeping your work safe ​

Your prose is plain markdown files in the story's Manuscript/ folder, and Leathercase guards them in several ways.

What could go wrongWhat Leathercase does
The app or the computer stops mid-saveEach save writes a new file, puts it on the disk, then swaps it in. The scene holds its old text or its new one, never half, never an empty file
Another app changes a scene you have openNothing is written over it; you choose Keep mine or Take the other
You close the window or quit with words not yet savedThe window waits until every scene is saved. A scene whose file changed in another app, still waiting on your choice, has what you typed kept in the story's history as <scene> <moment> not saved.md
A save writes over an earlier versionThe scene's text as it was goes into the story's history, at most once every ten minutes while you write, kept 30 days; the newest copy of each scene stays
A /scene, /chapter, /act, rename or undo rewrites a sceneEvery text it writes over or takes away goes into the history first, however recently one was kept. The text after the cursor is written into the new scene before it leaves the old one
You cut a sentence you later wantThe sentence log keeps every sentence you changed or cut, with what replaced it
You imported a storyThe originals in Word, Scrivener or your own notes are never touched, so the story as it was before you wrote here is always there

The history is the .history/Manuscript/ folder in the story's author data folder, plain markdown files any editor opens; to go back, open one and copy what you need. See Kept versions.

What Leathercase cannot do is keep a copy off this computer: it sends nothing anywhere. If the disk fails, the book goes with it unless your writing home is also somewhere else. Keep the home in a folder that syncs (Dropbox, iCloud Drive, OneDrive, Syncthing) or in git, or copy it to another drive now and then.

Not yet ​

  • The Graph does not write scenes: it holds the story's people, places, events and ties, not planned scenes.
  • The scenes the plot grid and the beat board write cannot be undone from the editor. Writing them only adds headings and files, never takes your text away; to take one back, delete it in your file manager.
  • Agents never write the Manuscript/ folder. A connected agent reads it, and any change it asks for there is refused.
