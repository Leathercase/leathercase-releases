Graph ​

The Graph is the whole story as one network: every character, place, item, faction and event, and the ties you draw between them. Leathercase reads it with graph theory and says what it finds in plain words: who holds the story together, where one tie is the only link between two groups, which triangles are under strain. Each tab is a view you make of the same network, from nothing or from a template such as Truby's character web. No scenes take part: the Graph is the story's structure, and when things happen belongs to the Plot grid.

Opening the Graph ​

Open a story, then pick Graph under the story's name in the sidebar, press Ctrl+6 (Cmd+6 on macOS), or type "Graph" in the command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K). It fits itself to the window the first time a tab is drawn; 0 fits it again.

Names ​

Every name in the story is in the Graph, whatever its kind. A small mark says the kind by its shape, so colour stays free for what you made:

MarkKind
CircleCharacter
SquarePlace
DiamondItem, or a kind of your own such as a symbol
HexagonFaction
TriangleEvent
Dashed circleA name with no note: found in the prose, or named only by your ties

A name's size follows a measure you pick in the tab's settings: Most ties (the default), Holds the story together, or All the same. Zoomed far out, only the names you point at keep their labels.

Ties ​

A tie joins two names. It is yours: Leathercase never draws one for you. Its side is its colour, and its type can add a stroke or an arrow:

LineTie
Inkon the same side: friends, same side, allied, or any tie you mark so
Vermilionat odds: a type at odds, or any tie you mark so
Two strokes, in its side's colourfamily
Half one colour, half the other, a small ring in the middlelooks one way, is another: a tie that looks friendly and is not, or the reverse
Thin grey, with an arrowany other type with a direction: works for, owns, wants
Thin greya type with no side and no direction, or a tie with no type
Dasheda tie an agent proposed, waiting in the inbox: the only dashed line

The legend at the bottom left of the canvas draws each of these with the line itself, and says that a name with a dotted ring has no note yet.

A tie's word, "her brother", is written level on its line, slid along it until it clears the names and the other words. In a tab with thirty ties or fewer every line that has room carries its word; with more, a word shows when you point at the tie or one of its names, when it is pinned, or when it lies on a path, and zoomed in close every line that has room shows it again. A link one note makes to another is no tie: it shows as a thin dotted line only around the name you point at or pin, and is never counted.

A relationship you wrote before the Graph existed, a ## Relationships line in a note or a line drawn on the old story map, is shown as a tie with its word and no type. Change it in the Graph and it moves into the Graph's file; your note stays as it was.

Cards at the pointer, and the side column ​

Point at a name and a card says its kind and group, its ties with their words, the other groups it reaches and through whom, how much it holds the story together, and the tabs that show it. Point at a tie and its card says its type and word, how it looks and what it is, where it sits among the groups, the pressure point it makes, and where the truth comes out.

Click to pin a name or a tie to the side column (toggle it with Ctrl/Cmd+B), which keeps the full list and the actions. Click again, click the background or press Esc to let go. Double-click a name to open its note in your writing software.

A pinned name shows its group, its rank in holding the story together, its roles in this tab, every tie (click one to pin it), and Open note, Focus, Paths and Connect…. For a name with no note: Another name for… and Not a name, kept in the story's settings. For a character: the pronoun. When Leathercase keeps a note for the name, its text is there to fill.

A pinned tie can be changed in place: its type, its word, its side; Looks one way, is another… gives it two states, Looks and Is, and Revealed links it to the Plot grid row where the reader learns the truth. Remove the tie takes its line out of the file; a tie written in a note offers Open the note instead, since the line is yours.

Making a tie ​

Drag from the ring around a name (it shows when you point at the name) and let go on another name. Or pin a name, press C or click Connect…, and click the other. A picker opens there:

  • The tab's tie types first, from its template, each with what it means: same side, at odds, side optional, or the direction it reads.
  • 1 to 5 picks one of the first five; + more shows the rest.
  • Or a type of your own: any word. It is kept as a new type, never refused.
  • In a word, optional: what the tie is, "the friend who stayed".
  • X or ⇄ flips the direction; Enter saves; Esc drops it.

The tie is saved as one line in Graph/Ties.md in the story's suite/ folder. The status line says what was written, with Undo and Redo.

The shape of the story ​

With nothing pinned, the side column describes the tab, counted on your ties only:

LineWhat it says
NamesHow many of each kind
GroupsHow many, and what they come from
PartsOne when every name reaches every other; otherwise how many parts with no tie between them
Holds the story togetherThe names on most of the shortest ways between two others, with the share
Most tiesThe names tied to the most others
Only link between two groupsA single tie that is all that joins two groups, and groups that never meet
Pressure pointsThree names where one is on the same side as two who are at odds. A triangle all at odds is not one: that is opposition working. One that holds only while a tie still looks friendly is marked as seeming so, with a dotted ring.
No tiesThe names with none

Each line names what it counted. Every name in it is a way in: click it to pin it.

A pressure point is also marked on the canvas: a "!" at the end of the label of the name pulled two ways. Point at it and a card says what it is, lists the three ties with their words, and says what such a triangle does. Its three names and ties light up while you point. Click the "!" to pin the name.

Groups ​

The Groups layout draws each group as a quiet area and keeps the areas apart, so a tie between groups runs long and shows where the story joins. Groups come from what the tab's Group by says:

Group byGroups
The tiesFound from your ties, each named for its most tied name until you name it
ThreadsThe thread of your Story threads a name is on the page in most; its other threads are listed with it
Plot grid strandsThe same, from the plot grid's columns
TagsA name's tags
Factions and householdsThe faction a name is tied to most
PlacesThe place a name is tied to most
By handThe groups you named, the rest ungrouped

Click a group's area to pin it: give it a name (the groups are then kept in the tab's file, so they stay put), or fold it. Find the groups again from the ties forgets the kept ones.

A folded group is one node with its count; its ties to the rest merge into one line per name, as wide as the ties it holds. Z folds or unfolds the pinned name's group, Shift+Z folds every group, double-click a folded group to open it. Past sixty names, groups open folded; a tab can turn that off. Folding is a view: nothing in Ties.md changes.

Names with no tie are set apart, in an area or a strip of their own, No ties yet, in every layout but the matrix, so none sits on a line it is no part of.

Backdrop draws a faint band between names that share two threads, strands or tags or more. It replaces the old map's shared-scene lines: a long book has a handful of threads, and hundreds of scenes.

Paths ​

Pin a name and press P, or click Paths, then click another name. Every shortest way between the two lights up, with the words on each tie. The column lists the ways with the types of their ties, who sits on most of them, and what is left without that one. Click a name to change the end; Shift+click to change the start. Every tie counts as one step, whatever its type; the list prints the types so you can judge the route.

Display and forces ​

The sliders icon at the top right of the canvas opens Display and forces, stacked like the graph settings of Obsidian. Each tab keeps its own, in its file.

SliderWhat it doesLayouts
Name scaleDraws the marks and labels larger or smaller, on top of the size the tab sets by a measureAll
SpacingSpreads the whole layout out, or draws it inAll
Tie lengthHow far apart tied names sit; around one name, how far apart the rings areFree, Around one name
Centre pullHow tightly names gather in the middleFree

The canvas follows a slider as you move it, and the tab's file is written once it rests: spacing: 1.6, tie length: 0.8, centre pull: 2, name scale: 1.2, only for those moved off 1. A slider the layout does not use is greyed out and says where it acts. Double-click a slider to set it back; Back to the defaults sets them all. A click outside the panel or Esc closes it. Names you dragged stay where you put them. Press 0 to fit the graph again after spreading it.

Drag a name to move it; it stays where you put it, kept beside the tab. Forget where I put names in the ⋯ menu lets them go.

Tabs and templates ​

Whole story is always the first tab. The + at the end of the tab row makes a new one, blank or from a template; the preview says what the template brings: the kinds shown, roles, tie types, layout and checks. Seven templates come with the app:

TemplateBrings
BlankNothing set
StoryEvery kind and the common tie types
Truby · character webHero, main opponent, second opponent, ally, fake ally, fake opponent and subplot; same side, at odds, looks one way and is another, family; four corners
Truby · symbol webStory symbols and character symbols; stands for, echoes, turns into
FamilyParent of, partner, sibling, estranged
Factions and allegianceLeader, member, informer, neutral; member of, allied, at war, pays, informs on; two sides
MysteryVictim, detective, suspect, culprit, witness; motive, alibi, clue to, lies about, saw

A tab keeps a copy of what it uses, so editing a template never rewrites a tab. Your own templates, and the built-in ones you change, are kept as markdown in Writer/Templates/ of the home, so they serve every story. Templates… in the ⋯ menu, or Edit template… in a tab's settings, opens the editor: the roles, the tie types and how each is drawn, what a tab shows, its layout and its checks, with the file it writes.

In a tab with roles, pin a name and give it a role, or use the Roles section of the column. In four corners each corner's Values, Weakness and Attacks by are kept with the tab, and each name's role is written above it. The template's checks are written as facts, never as instructions: "5 of 6 pairs of corners are at odds", "Nobody holds: subplot".

A symbol's note can say what it stands for: a stands-for: property with a link, or a list under a "Stands for" heading, - [[Nora Vidal]] — the truth she will not leave. The Graph reads it as a tie of yours and never writes into the note.

What an agent laid out ​

A tie an agent proposed, or a change to one, that is still waiting in the inbox is drawn dashed between the two names, with its word in italics: the only dashed line on the canvas. A note or a tie you accepted from an agent keeps a grey dashed outline, and its hover says which agent wrote it, until you change it. In Ties.md such a tie carries a by: line under it, which goes the first time you change the tie in the app.

An agent with the Lay out toolset reads the Graph as you see it, tab by tab, with the same measures, and may propose ties, changes to ties (a type for a relationship written before the Graph, a reveal), tabs and roles. See Connect an agent and the inbox.

The files, for editing outside the app ​

Everything the Graph adds is plain markdown with wiki links, readable and editable in any editor. The app reads it back when you save: a change made in another editor shows within a second.

markdown
# Ties

- [[Sabela Vidal]] family [[Nora Vidal]] — mother
  side: at odds
- [[Nora Vidal]] family [[Tomás Vidal]] — her brother
  looks: friendly
  is: at odds
  revealed: [[Plot grid#The confession]]
- [[Tomás Vidal]] works for [[Kell Salvage]] — crews for him, in secret
WhatWhere
Every tie, onceGraph/Ties.md in the story's suite/ folder
A tab's roles, corner values, groups and choicesGraph/<tab>.md beside it
Where you put names in a tabGraph/<tab>.layout.json; delete it and the layout is worked out again
Your templatesWriter/Templates/ of the home
Other names, ignored names, pronounsThe story's settings, Story.json

Leathercase rewrites only the lines of the tie you change, and keeps order, blank lines and comments as you left them. A type no template knows is kept. A line it cannot read is listed under Needs checking with its line number, never dropped. Your notes and chapters are never written. See Files Leathercase writes.
