The mate ​

The mate is Leathercase's own assistant. It reads your stories with Leathercase's rules, on this computer, and tells you once what is new: a scene with no point of view, a plant with no payoff, a thread stop whose quote has gone. It also answers "how do I" questions from the find field, suggests the next command worth trying, walks you through the sample, and, once you allow it, searches your stories by meaning. This page covers everything the mate does today, where it speaks, and how to quiet it.

The mate never writes your prose, and in the free app it wakes no language model at all. Everything on this page runs offline.

What the mate is, and what it is not ​

The mate is a set of Leathercase's own checks that run by themselves, plus a few helpers around the find field. It is not a chatbot.

The mate doesThe mate does not
Read the open story after a save, every half hour, and when you askWrite, rewrite or correct a word of your manuscript
Point at gaps Leathercase can see: empty cells, broken anchors, plants with no payoff, echoes, styleCall a language model (model turns are not available in the free app)
Tell you what is new once, as a count on its button and a list in its panelRepeat itself, or speak when nothing is new
Leave findings beside your scenes, which you can dismissChange your plot grid, board or comments on its own
Answer "how do I" from the app's commands, its guide and its skillsSend your text anywhere
Search by meaning with a small model on this computer, once you download itDownload anything without your word

The mate reaches your stories through the same door as any connected agent, under the same rules (see Connect an agent), and its calls are written to its own log. Unlike an outside agent, it can do nothing outside Leathercase.

Model turns come later

Settings shows a Model turns group with the line "Model turns come with a later paid service. Until then the mate wakes no model." If you want a language model to read your story today, connect your own agent, such as Claude Desktop or Claude Code. See Connect an agent.

Opening the mate ​

The mate has its own panel at the right edge of the window, beside whatever the view's side column shows, so what the mate says never mixes with what the view is for. There are three ways to open and close it:

  • The The mate button at the right end of the title bar, with a small pulse mark. It appears once a writing home is open.
  • Press Ctrl+Shift+M (Cmd+Shift+M on macOS). The command is Show or hide the mate, also in the View menu.
  • Click the mate's dashed line at the foot of the sidebar, above Settings.

Close it the same way, or with the × button (Close the mate) in the panel's header. Leathercase remembers whether the panel was open the next time you start it.

The button's count ​

When the mate has something new in the open story, its title bar button shows a count, for example The mate 2. Hover the button and its hint names the command, says how many and since when, and gives the key that opens it, such as "Show or hide the mate · 2 new in this story since 14:20 · Ctrl+Shift+M". When the mate is turned off, the hint says "off". When you have set Notifications to Off in Settings, the count is not shown.

The count is the one place the mate speaks outside its own panel. It never shows a pop-up, never plays a sound, and never interrupts your typing.

The sidebar line ​

At the foot of the sidebar the mate keeps one dashed line, its heartbeat:

The line saysMeaning
the mate · watchingIt is on and has not told anything in this home yet
the mate · 3 new · ticked 14:20Three new things across this home's stories; it last told something at 14:20
the mate · nothing new · ticked 14:20You have seen everything it found
the mate · offThe heartbeat is turned off in Settings

While the meaning model downloads, a second line under it shows the progress, for example "Meaning search · 42%", "Meaning search · paused at 42%" or "Meaning search · reading 2 of 4".

When the mate looks ​

The mate's heartbeat reads the open story when:

  • you open a story;
  • you save a file of the story in your own writing software (it waits three seconds after the last save);
  • you change the story in Leathercase: write in the plot grid, edit a thread or anchor a stop, put a beat card on the plot grid, accept a changeset (it looks again a moment after);
  • every thirty minutes while Leathercase is open;
  • you ask: Look now in its panel, or the command Run the mate now (in the Story menu and the find field, with a story open).

A look that finds nothing new writes nothing and says nothing. A look that finds new things tells at most eight of them per story, so the mate stays quiet even on a story that has many gaps.

The first six of these come from the plot grid and the threads; see Plot grid and Threads. Pace comes from the story's goals in Story settings. Echoes and style are the same checks as the manuscript's lenses; see Lenses and style checks.

Things tied to one scene become findings that stand beside that scene. Things about the whole story (pace, a character's pronoun, a stale changeset, a stop that links nowhere) are notices in the mate's panel only.

The mate's panel ​

The panel speaks only of the story that is open. From top to bottom:

  1. The header. The mate's name, how many things are new and since when ("2 new since 14:20"), or "nothing new", and the close button.
  2. First reading. The first time the mate reads a story, it says once how many things it found ("5 things to point at on the first reading.") and, under Missing, one step each, one line per gap, such as "Scenes with no point of view · 4", with a Run: button that opens the view where you would fill it. A faint line says "after this it speaks only when something is new".
  3. What is new. Each new thing as one sentence. A finding tied to a scene has an Open button with the scene's name, which opens the manuscript at that scene, its heading at the top and its first sentence selected. A finding from a rule has Mute this rule.
  4. Nothing new. When nothing is new, the panel says "Nothing new since the last look."
  5. What was seen · N. A link that folds out what the mate told before and you have already seen.
  6. Look now. Runs the heartbeat on the open story at once.
  7. Not tried yet. One suggestion at a time: a command you have not used yet, with its key, a Try it button and Don't suggest this. See Next steps.
  8. Every finding, in the inbox. At the panel's foot, a link to the inbox's Findings tab, where every finding of every story in the home is listed.

When the mate is turned off, the panel says "The mate is off. Preferences › The mate turns it on."

Muting a rule ​

Mute this rule turns that rule off at once, for every story, and the panel says, for example, "Echoes muted. Preferences › The mate turns it back on." Turn it back on in Settings › The mate › What it watches.

Findings beside your scenes ​

A finding the mate leaves is drawn on your views in the assistant's style, with its own tag: the pulse mark and the words "the mate". You meet them:

  • under a scene's heading in the manuscript;
  • in the Proposals sheet of a story view's side column, under Findings and comments (open it with Ctrl+Shift+.);
  • in the inbox's Findings tab.

Each finding offers Open the scene, Dismiss, and for the mate's own findings Mute this rule. A finding is never written into your files: it lives in Leathercase's own assistant data, in the story's suite/assistant/ folder.

Dismiss hides a finding. The mate does not raise it again unless the scene it is about changes. When the cause of a finding goes away, for example you fill the empty point-of-view cell or anchor a lost stop to its new passage, the mate withdraws the finding itself. A fix made in Leathercase counts at once: the mate looks again a moment after, with no save in your own files needed.

The assistant's layer can be hidden with Ctrl+., the command Show or hide the assistant's layer. That hides the mate's findings on the views along with what agents proposed. See Proposals and the inbox.

Findings in the inbox ​

The inbox, Ctrl+0, has two tabs at its top: Changesets and Findings, the second with a count of what is new. The Findings tab lists what the mate told about each story in the home, newest first, grouped by story ("The Lamp at Ferrol · 3").

  • New (the default) shows only what is new since you last looked; All shows everything kept.
  • Each line gives the sentence, the rule's name, the time, and a dashed new tag on what was new when you came in.
  • Open with a scene's name opens that scene in the manuscript.

Opening the Findings tab counts as looking: the counts on the mate's button and sidebar line start again from zero. Empty, the tab says "Nothing new from the mate." or, under All, "The mate has told nothing yet."

The mate in the find field ​

The find field in the title bar (Ctrl+K or Ctrl+O) is described in Find, open and make. The mate adds three things under it.

Ask "how do I" ​

Type a question, starting with "how do I", "how to", "how can I", or ending with a question mark. The mate answers under How do I from the app's commands, its guide pages and its skills:

  • numbered steps;
  • a button Run: followed by the command's name, and the command's key when it has one;
  • a faint line saying where the answer came from: "from the commands", "from the guide" or "from the skills".

When nothing matches, it says so plainly, "Nothing on this computer answers that.", and offers the Nearest command as a button. The answer is found on this computer; no model is asked.

Next steps ​

With nothing typed, the find field shows one step you have not tried yet under Not tried yet, the same suggestion as in the mate's panel, with Try it and Don't suggest this. A step you dismiss is not suggested again. Suggestions come one at a time, from commands you have never used, and never while you type.

Leathercase counts which commands you have used so it knows what to suggest. Those counts stay in the app's data on this computer; they are never synced and never sent.

Turn suggestions off in Settings › The mate › Next steps.

Search your stories ​

Type three or more letters (not a question, and with no colon, which makes a note) and the find field also searches the passages of every story in the open home. Word matches come first under Words · N, with the words you typed highlighted and a words tag. Words match whole, the last one also by its beginning, and passages holding your words in the order you typed them come first. Only your scenes and sentence logs are searched, never Leathercase's own files or the daily snapshots. Pick one to open the passage in its story's manuscript. See Search your stories' text.

When no passage has your words, it says "No passage has these words."

Meaning search ​

Word search finds the words you type. Meaning search finds passages that say the same thing in other words, in any language your stories use: "lighthouse keeper" can find the scene where Nora climbs the tower, and a Portuguese query can find an English scene.

It needs a small model, Xenova/multilingual-e5-small (MIT licence, about 135 MB), downloaded once. After that it runs on this computer, and your text never leaves it.

Turning it on ​

Leathercase asks once. The first time a search in the find field finds no words, a box headed Search by meaning ("not on this computer yet") explains what meaning search does and how big the model is, and offers two buttons:

  • Download, 135 MB fetches the model. The download is checked file by file before it is used.
  • Not now keeps word search. Leathercase does not ask again for a week.

You can also download or remove the model at any time in Settings › Search.

While it downloads, the find field and the sidebar line show the progress, with Pause, Resume and Cancel. Word search works meanwhile. When the download ends, Leathercase reads your stories once to prepare them ("Then it reads your stories once", "2 of 4 stories").

If the download fails, the box says "The download did not finish:" and the reason, with Try again.

Searching by meaning ​

Once the model is on this computer, a switch appears above the results, Search by: Words and meaning (the default), Words, or Meaning.

  • Word matches come first, "exact, first".
  • Meaning matches follow under Meaning · N, "other words, closest first", each tagged meaning · close or meaning · related, and with the passage's language when it differs.
  • A faint line at the foot says "meaning runs on this computer".

On an Intel Mac, meaning search is not available: Settings says "This computer cannot run it: there is no runtime for Intel Macs. Word search works as always."

Start here, on the sample ​

When you open the sample from first run (Try the sample story first), a column called Start here stands beside the Library page. A dashed note at its top says it is the sample: short stories, one in each format Leathercase reads, copied into the app's data. Change anything; none of it is yours.

It lists six things to do once, each ticked by doing it, never by a checkbox:

  1. Plot grid
  2. Manuscript
  3. Threads
  4. Find a story, a view or a command
  5. Beat board
  6. Declare a story

A count says how many are done ("1 of 6"). The next step is drawn in a box with a Show me button that runs it. Each step shows its key when it has one.

At its foot:

  • Hide puts Start here away. It stays in the find field: open the field with nothing typed and pick Start here.
  • Start over makes a fresh copy of the sample, every change undone, and keeps Start here open.
  • Leave the sample forgets the sample and deletes its copy, with nothing left behind. If it was your only home, the first-run page comes back.

Notifications ​

By default the mate tells you only inside the app. In Settings › The mate › How it tells you, Notifications has three choices:

ChoiceWhat happens
OffNo count on the button. The panel and the inbox still list what is new
In the app (default)The count on the button and the sidebar line
Also the system, when awayAs above, and a system notification when Leathercase's window is in the background

System notifications are always silent, come at most once every three hours, and never during quiet hours. Choosing Also the system, when away shows two more rows:

  • What may reach the system: a switch per rule. By default only Changesets gone stale, Pace behind the goal and Thread stops that lost their quote may.
  • Quiet hours: From and To times (22:00 to 08:00 by default), or None.

Model text and the gate ​

Leathercase has one rule for text a model writes: it is shown to you in an editable box, scrubbed of hidden characters, and kept only when you accept it. The mate writes no model text today, since it wakes no model. The rule is already in force for connected agents: every text an agent sends (a card's title, a cell, a comment, a reason) has hidden characters such as zero-width spaces and direction marks taken out before you see it, can be edited in the box before you accept it, and changes nothing until you do. See Proposals and the inbox.
