The Desk ​

The Desk is the page that answers three questions each day, across every story in your writing home: did I write, am I on pace, and what needs checking. It is a desk, not a dashboard: a sentence about today, a card for each story with a goal, the files that changed, and a short list of things to look at. This page explains what each part shows, how the words are counted, and the heartbeat in the title bar that tells you when an open story was last read.

Open the Desk ​

Click Desk under Home in the sidebar, or press Ctrl+2 (Cmd+2 on macOS). You can also type desk in the find field. The sidebar holds Library (Ctrl+1), Desk (Ctrl+2), Writer (Ctrl+3), Inbox (Ctrl+0) and Tags (Ctrl+8).

How the words are counted ​

There is no editor in Leathercase, so it cannot count keystrokes. It counts words by reading your files: each reading compares every file's words with the last reading and records what was added and what was cut, file by file and scene by scene, in the story's writing log. So Leathercase knows when each scene last changed, and a connected agent can tell you the scene you worked on last; for work from before Leathercase first read the story, it goes by the chapter file's date. The log lives in the story's suite/logs/writing/ folder, one small file per day and per computer, so it travels with the story (see Files Leathercase writes).

Three things follow from that:

  • The first reading only sets the baseline. A story read for the first time has nothing to compare with, so its words are its starting point, not words written today. Until a second reading sees a change, the Desk says No change has been seen yet: the first reading of each file only sets its baseline.
  • Words are counted when Leathercase reads. While the Desk is open, it follows the folder of every story in the home and reads again a moment after any file is saved, so the count moves without reopening anything. Elsewhere in the app, only the open story is followed. A change made while Leathercase was closed is counted the next time it reads that story, on the day it reads it.
  • Cutting counts as work. Words cut are kept beside words added, never netted against them. A day of cutting shows as a day of cutting, not as a day off.

Opening the Desk reads every story of the home once. While it reads, the right-hand column says Reading every story….

A copy a day

When the Desk opens, it also keeps a copy of Leathercase's own files for the day, in suite/daily/, so a bad sync or a slip on the board can be undone by hand. Fourteen days are kept, and your manuscripts are never copied: your own tools keep those. See Files Leathercase writes.

Today, in one sentence ​

The top of the Desk gives the date, then one sentence about today:

What happened todayThe sentence
Words were addedYou wrote 412 words today, in The Lamp at Ferrol. (every story with words added is named)
Words were only cutYou cut 120 words today, in The Salt Road. Cutting counts as work.
Nothing changedNo changes in the manuscripts yet today.

The line under it says how the count is kept on a day of work (Words are counted by reading your files; the count updates when a file is saved.), names your last writing on a quiet day (Last writing: The Lamp at Ferrol, Tuesday, 380 words.), or, before any story has been read, Nothing has been read yet: open a story to take its first reading.

The Desk keeps no streak. A missed day is not counted against you.

Goal cards ​

Each story with a goal (a word target, a number of words a day, or both) gets a card, the story you worked on most recently first.

A goal card shows, from top to bottom:

  • The story's name. Click it to open the story.
  • The target, such as 60,000 words by December 31, or the number of words alone when there is no deadline.
  • The verdict, with a coloured dot (see the table below).
  • A bar towards the target, with the words the story holds now on the left and the pace on the right: 13,790 to go · 222 a day for 62 days, target met, or the deadline has passed.
  • The last fourteen days, one bar per day, today last and marked. A bar's height is the words added that day. A day you only cut shows as a flat mark. When there is a deadline or a daily number, a dashed line shows the words a day the goal needs. Point at a bar to read the day: Sep 16: 300 added, 20 cut.

A story with only a daily goal, set with Words a day, has a smaller card: its line says the number, such as 300 words a day, with no verdict, no bar towards a target and no countdown. Its fourteen days are drawn against the dashed line of that daily number.

The words a day needed are worked out over the days you write. If the story's settings say you write on weekdays only, the same target asks more words on fewer days.

The verdicts ​

VerdictWhen
Not read yetLeathercase has never read the story's files
Target metThe story holds as many words as its target
Deadline passedThe deadline is behind you and the target is not met
StalledNothing has been added or cut for more than seven days
No targetThe story has no word target
No deadlineA target with no deadline
On paceThe last seven days, on average, cover the words a day the deadline asks
BehindThe last seven days fall short of it

Green is on pace or met, amber is behind or past the deadline, grey is everything else. The same verdict shows beside the story in the Library and in the find field.

Set a goal ​

When no story in the home has a goal, the Desk offers Set a goal instead of goal cards. A goal is optional and belongs to one story; it is kept in that story's settings.

  1. Choose the kind of goal: Draft by a date (a word target and a deadline; the pace is worked out for you) or Words a day (a daily number with no end date).
  2. Choose the Story.
  3. For a draft, type the Target and pick a Deadline, or leave the date empty for a target with no deadline. Leathercase suggests a round target above what the story holds now, and says how many words are to go. For words a day, type the number; 500 is suggested.
  4. Click Set goal.

You can set or change a goal any time in Story settings. The keys writing-target, writing-deadline and writing-daily in a story's note are read when you declare it (see Scaffold a story folder).

Changed in the manuscripts today ​

Under the goal cards, Changed in the manuscripts today lists every file of every story that gained or lost words today, the most added first. Each row shows the file's name, the story and the file's path, and +412 for words added or −120 for words cut; a file that had both says how many were cut beside it. Click a row to open its story.

Revision work ​

When a story's Sentence log kept sentences today, Revision work lists the story with 3 sentences logged today: the sentences you rewrote or cut. Click it to open the story.

Needs checking ​

The right-hand column says what needs a look across the home. When all is well it says Nothing needs checking: every quoted anchor matches its prose and every story's folder was found. Otherwise it counts the items, Needs checking · 2, and lists each with a button that fixes or explains it:

ItemWhat it meansThe button
3 anchors no longer match their proseA thread stop or comment quotes a sentence that is no longer in its scene, usually because you rewrote it. The story and the scene are namedReview opens the story's manuscript on the review of its broken anchors
The story's folder was not foundThe folder was moved, renamed or is on a drive that is not mountedFind it asks where the folder lives now
2 conflicted copies of Leathercase's filesA sync tool left two versions of one of Leathercase's filesResolve opens Settings, Storage, where you choose which to keep

See Comments and word lists, Threads, Manuscript and Homes and stories for each.

Where you left off ​

When you have selected a passage in a story's manuscript, the Desk shows it under Where you left off: the story, the scene, when you selected it, and the passage itself in quotation marks. Click it to open the manuscript there. The Library shows the same passage.

The heartbeat in the title bar ​

While a story is open, the right end of the title bar shows when Leathercase last read it: a dot and read just now, read 3 min ago and so on. A small number beside it counts the readings that found a change since you last looked.

Click it to open the feed. Its first line says Read just now from 28 files. and how the folder is followed:

DotWhat it says
WatchingThe system tells Leathercase of each save, and a sweep confirms it now and then.
PollingThe system cannot tell of saves here, so Leathercase looks for them every few seconds. This happens on some network and synced drives
LostThe story's folder cannot be read. The views show the last reading.

Below it, each reading that found something has its own entry, newest first: Saved · 2026-10-06 21:14, or, for the first reading after the story is opened, While it was closed · since the time of the reading before, with what changed in between, whatever program made the change. Each entry lists what that reading found:

  • New scene: The tower, 412 words, Scene gone:, Renamed: The cottage → The keeper's cottage
  • The cottage: +86 words
  • New in the story: Inés Roca, A new name in the prose: Michaelmas, No longer in the story:
  • Nora Vidal now in The tower, no longer in
  • A thread whose stop now points at no scene, lost its quote, or pays off before its promise, and is fine again when that is mended

When the feed is empty it says Nothing has changed in the story since it was last read. The last line sums up the latest save: Last change: 86 words written, 12 cut. The feed keeps the last twenty readings for as long as the story stays open.

After a save, the scenes and people it touched are marked in the views for a few seconds, then the marks fade.
