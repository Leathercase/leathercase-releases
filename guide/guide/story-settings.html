Story settings ​

A story's settings say where the story lives, which of its files are read, what the Desk counts it toward, how its prose marks speech and thought, which words to watch, which names are not people, and which threads do which job on the plot grid. They travel with the story: they are kept in the story's own folder, in suite/Story.json, so a story synced to another computer brings them along. This page goes through every field and what it changes.

Opening the settings ​

With a story open, any of these:

  • The story's ⋯ menu in the sidebar (hover the story's name): Story settings.
  • The breadcrumb: click the view's name after the story and pick Story settings at the bottom of the menu, or open the story's own menu in the breadcrumb.
  • The command palette (Ctrl+K, or Cmd+K on macOS): Story settings.
  • Settings, then the story in the Story list on the left, Structure and goals, and Open the story's settings.

The page replaces the view, beside the story, so what Leathercase read of it is at hand. Back to the story at the foot of the section list, or Esc, returns to the view you came from.

How the page works ​

The page has seven sections, listed on the left under the story's name: Story, Goal, Prose, Words, Characters, Graph and Plot grid. Some show a figure beside their name: the number of files the story reads, the target, the story's language (EN), the number of word lists, the number of names that are not people, and how many of the grid's four jobs are set (2/4).

Each section has a title, the file it writes at the top right, and a lead paragraph. Every change is written at once, as on the app's own settings pages: a text field when you leave it or press Enter, a list or a button the moment you pick it. A change to the folder or the file patterns reads the story again.

A change the story cannot take is refused, with the reason in rose, and not written:

  • A story needs a name.
  • A story needs at least one pattern, such as **/*.md.

Story ​

"Where the story lives in the home and which of its files are read. A change here reads the folder again."

FieldWhat it does
NameThe story's name, as the sidebar, the Library and the title page show it. Renaming it leaves the folder and your files as they are.
Folder under the libraryThe folder, inside the home, that holds the story, such as The Lamp at Ferrol. Empty means the whole home is the story.
Mark"How the story looks in the sidebar and the Library": a row of icons and a row of colours to pick from; the story's mark in the sidebar changes as you pick.
FilesHow many files match (42 files match), and how many the leave-out patterns take away (· 3 left out). Show opens the story's folder as a tree with a tick for each file; Hide folds it. How it is read opens the page that shows what Leathercase made of every file.

The file tree ​

Show lists every file of the story's folder. A ticked file is read; an unticked one is not. Tick or untick a file (Read 01 The Night the Lamp Went Out.md) or a whole folder (Read the folder Act I - The Dark Lamp), and Leathercase turns your ticks into patterns for you. The count above reads 38 of 42 files; Clear unticks everything (the story needs at least one file to save).

Beside each folder, a list says what it holds:

ChoiceMeans
The story's proseChapters and scenes. Only the prose makes scenes.
Characters, Places, Items, Factions, EventsNotes about the story's world, one kind each.
Plans (outline, synopsis), Records (chronology, style sheet), Work notes (journal, feedback), Research, Scraps (cut scenes)Notes that are part of the story but not its prose.
A kind of your ownKinds you declared for the story.
As its folder, By its name: …, Not said: …Nothing said here: the folder above decides, or the folder's name does (Characters/ reads as characters), or the files wait unsorted until you say.

A note with its own type: in its front matter keeps that type whatever its folder says. The whole of this is explained in How a story is read and Declaring a story.

Advanced: the patterns ​

Under Advanced: the patterns, folded at first, the ticks are shown as the patterns they make. You can type them yourself, one per line:

FieldExampleWhat it does
Files to read, one pattern per line**/*.md reads every note.Which files of the folder belong to the story.
Files to leave out, one pattern per line_Work/**Which of those to leave out. To leave out the sample's _Work/ notes, for instance, add _Work/**.

* matches any name, ** any number of folders. The box is written when you leave it.

Under it, Leathercase's author data files for the story, each with a filled tick when it exists and, when it does not yet, when it is made:

FileMade
StoryThreads.mdmade on your first thread
PlotGrid.mdmade the first time the story is read

Goal ​

"What the desk counts toward. With no target, the story is read and mapped, not counted down."

FieldWhat it does
Target"The words the finished story should have." 0 for none.
Deadline"The day it should be done, if there is one." A date picker; clear it for no deadline.
A day"Words a writing day aims for; new stories start at 500." The starting figure comes from Settings › New stories.
Days you writeA button for each day of the week. "every day counts; click to keep only some." Turn days off and the pace and the words a day needed by the deadline count only the days left on. Turning all seven back on means every day.

Under the fields, a dashed box reads the goal back as one sentence, the way the Desk will count it. For example:

  • "60,000 words by 2026-12-31 is 640 a day from today, more than the 500 a day set. At the recent pace, done by 2027-03-14."
  • "500 a day, 5 days a week, reaches 60,000 in 110 sittings, around 2027-03-20."
  • "No target. 500 a day is the goal the desk counts against."
  • "No target and no daily goal: the story is read and mapped, not counted down."
  • "60,000 words: already there."
  • "60,000 words by 2026-09-30: the deadline has passed, 12,000 to go."

The last part, At the recent pace, done by …, appears once Leathercase has seen enough of your writing to project a finishing day. See The Desk.

Prose ​

"How the story is written, so the lenses read it right. Leathercase tells it from the page; confirm it or correct it."

Language ​

Language sets the story's language: "The book's words and the templates follow the story's language, not the app's." The first choice, Told from the prose: English (or Told from the prose (English until there is enough)), lets Leathercase tell it from your paragraphs. The others are English (United States), English (United Kingdom), English (Canada), Português (Brasil) and Português (Portugal).

It changes:

  • The words an export adds: Chapter One, Contents, by, END (see Export).
  • The language of the templates Leathercase offers for the story, such as the beat board's.
  • The heading of the sentence log as an agent reads it.

It never changes the app's own language, which is set in Settings › Appearance. See Languages.

Speech is written as ​

How your story marks speech, so the dialogue and accents lenses find it. Each choice shows a sample line:

ChoiceSample
As the prose readsLeathercase tells from the page
Double quotes“Not tonight,” she said.
Single quotes‘Not tonight,’ she said.
A dash— Not tonight, she said.
No speech marksShe said it was not tonight.

Leathercase reads your paragraphs and says what it found on the first row, such as double quotes, in 112 paragraphs (or nothing read that way yet). The convention it found is marked with its count and shows one of your own sentences; every other row shows that same sentence re-marked in its convention, so you see your own line each way. Hover a sample for "your own sentence" or "your sentence, re-marked".

Thoughts are written as ​

The same, for thought:

ChoiceSample
As the prose readsLeathercase tells from the page
An italic paragraphHe is up there. He has to be.
Italics anywhereShe looked up. He is up there. She waited.
Single quotes‘He is up there,’ she thought.
Not markedHe was up there, she knew.

The Lamp at Ferrol declares double quotes for speech and an italic paragraph for thought. How the lens reads each is on Lenses and style checks.

Speakers ​

Speakers, in the order the lens tries them narrows and orders the cast the dialogue lens gives lines to. Each speaker is a chip with its number, ← and → to move it earlier or later (Move Nora Vidal earlier), and × to remove it. Add one with + from the cast, which lists your characters not yet in, or type a name in + a name, then Enter. A name with no character note can be added too.

A speaker may carry a colour of its own after the name, Nora Vidal #1f5f7a. The accents lens marks that character's words in it; the dialogue lens does not colour lines by speaker, it tints speech and thought and names the speaker in the side column (see Lenses and style checks). Left empty, every character note is a speaker, in cast order.

Words ​

"The words to watch. Each change is written at once to Leathercase's file beside the story." This section edits the story's word lists in WordLists.md: each list as a row of chips, × to take a word out, and List, Word or phrase and Add to put one in. The words lens marks them on the manuscript page. Full details are on Comments and word lists.

How each character speaks (the words they use and never say) is not here: it is kept on each character's Voice tab in the characters view.

Characters ​

"The sections Leathercase adds, empty, to a character's note in Leathercase when you ask. Kept for every story of the home."

Character sections is a box with one section name per line ("one per line, for every story of the home"), and Save the sections. They are kept in suite/CharacterSections.md at the home's root, so every story of the home shares them. See Characters.

Graph ​

"Names the Graph found that are not people. They stay in your prose and leave the Graph."

Not people: names the Graph must not take lists the capitalised words the Graph must not take for names, such as a month or a word of address. The Lamp at Ferrol has Michaelmas and Pai.

  • Each is a chip with × to give it back to the Graph.
  • Below, dashed chips offer names found in the prose with no note of their own, with how often they appear (Michaelmas 4×). Click one to ignore it ("found in the prose with no note; click to ignore").
  • Type any other name in a name, then Enter.

The names stay in your prose; they only leave the Graph, the cast and the lists built from it. The same list can be undone from the Graph's Names you settled.

Plot grid ​

"Which headings of StoryThreads.md do which job on the plot grid."

Under The grid's jobs, from StoryThreads.md, four lists, each offering the headings of your threads file, or none:

FieldWhat the chosen heading becomes
POV columnThe grid's point-of-view column, read as whose scene it is.
Time columnThe grid's time column, read as when the scene happens.
Main themeThe thread the grid treats as the story's main theme.
Plot pointsThe thread the grid treats as the story's plot points.

The Lamp at Ferrol sets POV, Time and Theme: What do we owe the drowned?. A heading chosen here that is no longer in the file shows in the warning colour as POV (not in StoryThreads.md). With no heading in the file yet the section says: "No heading yet: the grid's column menu sets these when the threads file has some." The grid's own column menu sets the same four; see Plot grid.

What is not on this page ​

Some of a story's settings are kept in Story.json but set elsewhere, where they make sense:

SettingWhere
The app the story opens inSettings › Sources, or the chooser on the manuscript page
The pen name it is published underSettings › Export for the story (its Byline row)
Other names and pronouns confirmed for a characterThe Graph
The grid's column orderThe plot grid
The story's own kindsDeclaring a story

A story declared from a project note reads that note's keys once, when it is declared: The Lamp at Ferrol's writing-target, writing-daily, writing-deadline, dialogue, thoughts, speakers, plot-pov, plot-time, plot-theme and story-ignore filled these fields. After that, the settings page is where they change. See Front matter.
