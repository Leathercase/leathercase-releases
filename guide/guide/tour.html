A tour of the window ​

This page names every part of the Leathercase window and says what each control does: the title bar with its breadcrumb and find field, the sidebar, the menus, the side column, the heartbeat of the reading, hints, the theme, size and contrast, and the lines that report a problem. Learn the frame once and every view reads the same way.

Keys are written for Windows and Linux. On macOS, Cmd takes the place of Ctrl: Ctrl+K is Cmd+K. The app shows its keys with the Mac symbols on a Mac.

The parts at a glance ​

PartWhereWhat it is for
SidebarDown the left edge, full heightYour home, its places, its stories and the open story's views; Settings at its foot
Title barAcross the top, right of the sidebarThe sidebar button, the breadcrumb, the find field, and the reading's state
The viewThe middleWhat you are looking at: the Library, the Desk, a story's plot grid, and so on
Side columnThe right edge of a viewThe view's filters, details and sheets, beside the view and never over it
FooterUnder the viewThe keys this view answers to
The mate's panelThe far right, when openLeathercase's own assistant; see The mate

The window never scrolls as a whole. Each view scrolls its own body, so the title bar and the footer stay where they are.

The title bar ​

The title bar runs across the top of the window. From left to right it holds the sidebar button, the breadcrumb, the find field in the middle of the window, and at its right end the state of the folder or the story, the agents' indicator when an agent is connected, and the mate's button.

The sidebar button ​

The first button collapses the sidebar to a rail of icons, or opens it again. Rest the pointer on it to see its key: Sidebar · Ctrl \. See Collapsing the sidebar.

The breadcrumb ​

The breadcrumb says where you are, as a path:

Where you areThe breadcrumb reads
A place of the homeStories › Desk (your home's name, then the place)
Inside a storyStories › The Lamp at Ferrol › Plot grid
In the manuscript, scrolled downStories › The Lamp at Ferrol › Manuscript › Act II - Salvage › 05 The Reef
Declaring a storyStories › Declare a story

Each part does something:

  • The home's name goes back to the Library.
  • The story's name, with a small arrow, opens the Switch story menu. Its heading says Switch story, stay on Threads (or whichever view you are on): picking another story opens it on the same view. Each story has its state dot, the open one is marked current. Below the stories are Back to the Library (Ctrl+1) and the story's actions: Story settings, How this story is read, Open the story in your software (Ctrl+Shift+O) and Export the manuscript as one file (Ctrl+E).
  • The view's name, with a small arrow, opens the Switch view menu: Beat board, Plot grid, Threads, Characters, Graph and Manuscript, each with its key, the current one highlighted, then How this story is read and Story settings below a line.
  • In the manuscript, the parts after the view name the part and chapter at the top of the page. Click one to scroll back to its heading. Scrolled back to the top of the book, the path ends at Manuscript again.

When the window is too narrow for the whole path, the links in the middle fold into a … menu after the home's name; narrower still, the home folds in too. The story and view menus and the part you are on never fold. A name cut short shows in full when you rest the pointer on it.

The find field ​

The field in the middle of the title bar finds and makes. Its placeholder reads Find anything, or make it: character: Nora, and its key shows at its right end: Ctrl+K. Click it or press Ctrl+K and a list drops under it.

Type to find stories, views, commands, cards, tags, notes and, inside a story, its people, places and scenes. Type a word and a colon, such as character: Nora or tag: winter, to make one. With nothing typed it lists the commands you used recently, the home's stories and every command. The foot of the list reminds you of the keys: ↑ ↓ move, enter run, esc close, word: title makes it. When nothing matches it says Nothing matches. Esc closes.

The find field is the app's command palette too: every command in the menus can be run from it by name, with its key shown beside it. Ctrl+O (Find or make in this view) opens the same field. The full story is on Find, open and make.

Who carries the folder, or where the story is read from ​

At the right end of the title bar:

  • At a place of the home (Library, Desk, Writer, Tags, Inbox, Settings) it says who carries your home's folder: Local folder with a hollow dot, or Dropbox · detected with a dashed dot. Rest the pointer on it: No sync tool holds this folder., or Dropbox carries this folder; Leathercase never uploads anything itself. Leathercase tells Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud Drive and OneDrive from the folder's path alone.
  • Inside a story it shows the heartbeat (below) and, with a small lock, where the story is read from: Markdown · read-only, Obsidian · read-only, Word · read-only, Scrivener · read-only or Fountain · read-only. The lock is the promise that Leathercase never writes into it.

The agents' indicator and the mate's button ​

When an agent such as Claude Code is connected, an indicator appears before the state, saying who is connected and what it is reading. It is absent otherwise. See Connect an agent.

The last button, The mate, opens and closes the mate's panel (Ctrl+Shift+M). It shows a count when the mate has something new for the open story. See The mate.

The heartbeat ​

Inside a story, Leathercase follows the story's folder and reads it again each time a file in it is saved, by any app. The heartbeat in the title bar says when that last happened: read just now, read 12 s ago, read 3 min ago, read 2 h ago, read yesterday, or the date. While the first reading runs it says reading…. A small number beside it counts readings that found a change you have not looked at yet.

Its dot says how the folder is followed:

DotMeaning
WatchingThe system tells Leathercase of each save, and a sweep confirms it now and then.
PollingThe system cannot tell of saves here, so Leathercase looks for them every few seconds. This happens on some network and synced drives.
LostThe story's folder cannot be read. The views show the last reading. The folder was moved, renamed, or its drive is not mounted.

Click the heartbeat to open it. The top line repeats when and from how many files the story was read, for example Read just now from 14 files., and how the folder is followed. Below are the readings that changed something, newest first, up to twenty:

  • Saved · 2026-10-06 14:32, for a save while the story was open.
  • While it was closed · since 2026-10-05 21:10, for the first reading after you open a story: what changed while it was closed, whatever software made the change.

Each reading lists what it found changed in the story, in plain lines:

  • New scene: Three, 412 words, Scene gone: The Reef, Renamed: The Ledger → What the Ledger Said
  • 05 The Reef: +120 words
  • New in the story: Marta, No longer in the story: Pai, A new name in the prose: Rosalía
  • Nora Vidal now in The Storm Rises, Kell no longer in The Reef
  • For threads, Nora Vidal: the stop at The Lens points at no scene, and later Nora Vidal: the stop at The Lens is fine again

When nothing has changed it says Nothing has changed in the story since it was last read. The last line sums up the newest change: Last change: 412 words written, 30 cut. Opening the heartbeat clears its count. For a few seconds after a reading, the views mark the scenes and the people it moved.

The sidebar ​

The sidebar is the whole home in one column. It has the same anatomy everywhere, inside a story too.

The head: the brand and the home switcher ​

At the top sit Leathercase's Le icon and wordmark, then the home switcher: your home's tile, its name, and under it how many stories it has and who carries it, such as 4 stories · local folder or 2 stories · Dropbox. The tile shows the icon and colour you gave the home, or the first letter of its name when it has no icon. Rest the pointer on the switcher to read the home's folder. Click it to open the Switch home menu:

  • Each home known on this computer, with its tile and its folder, the open one ticked and marked current beside its folder. Two homes of the same name read apart by their folders.
  • Name and look…, which opens the home's name, colour and icon in Settings. See Homes and stories.
  • Add a writing home…, which opens your system's folder dialog. See Homes and stories.
  • While the sample is open: Start the sample over and Leave the sample.

Home ​

The first group, Home, lists the home's places, each with its key:

PlaceKeyWhat it is
LibraryCtrl+1Every story in the home, and the passage you last selected. See Homes and stories.
DeskCtrl+2What changed today across every story, and what needs you. See The Desk.
WriterCtrl+3The Writer Board of cards, shared by every story. See The Writer Board.
InboxCtrl+0Changes agents proposed, to review. Shows a count instead of its key when some wait. See Proposals and the inbox.
TagsCtrl+8Every tag in the home. See Tags.

The place you are on is highlighted.

Stories ​

The second group, Stories, lists the home's stories. Each row has the story's mark (the icon and colour you gave it when you declared it) with a small state dot on it, and its name. The state dot is green when the story keeps pace with its goal or met its target, amber when it is behind or past its deadline, and grey otherwise; rest the pointer on the row to read it, as in on pace or no target. See Verdicts for each one.

  • The plus beside Stories opens the journey that declares a story. See Declaring a story.
  • Click a story to open it. It expands into its views: Beat board, Plot grid, Threads, Characters, Graph and Manuscript, each with its key where it has one. Only the open story is expanded. Click another story to switch to it.
  • The ⋯ on the open story's row opens its menu: Story settings, How this story is read, Open the story in your software and Export the manuscript as one file. Export, here, in the breadcrumb's story menu and with Ctrl+E, goes straight to the save dialog with the settings already saved for the story; the export settings themselves are in Settings › Export for that story. See Export.

A story reopens on the view you last chose; the first time, on the Manuscript.

The foot ​

At the foot of the sidebar sits the mate's line, a dashed box that says the mate · nothing new, the mate · 2 new, the mate · watching or the mate · off, and when it last looked. Click it to open the mate's panel.

Under it is Settings (Ctrl+,). A small dot on it means a newer version of Leathercase is out; rest the pointer on the dot to read Version 0.31.0 is out. See Settings.

Collapsing the sidebar ​

Press Ctrl+\, click the sidebar button in the title bar, or choose Collapse or expand the sidebar from the View menu. The sidebar folds to a rail of icons:

  • the Le icon, and the home's initial for the switcher;
  • the home's places: Library, Desk, Writer, Inbox and Tags;
  • inside a story, a state dot for the open story and its six views as icons;
  • the mate's line hides; Settings stays at the foot.

Rest the pointer on an icon to see its name, such as Graph · The Lamp at Ferrol. Click it to go there. The choice to keep the sidebar collapsed is remembered on this computer.

Inside a view ​

Every view of a story, and the home's own Writer and Tags, shares one shell, so learning one teaches the others.

The head ​

A thin bar across the top of the view:

  • The state line, on the left, in small grey type, says what is shown and what is hidden: how many scenes, how many filtered out, and so on. It is read aloud by a screen reader when it changes.
  • reset appears beside it when a filter is on, and clears every filter at once.
  • N unsorted, in amber, appears when some of the story's files wait to be sorted. Click it to open How this story is read.
  • When agents proposed changes this view draws, a tag such as 3 proposed here opens them in the side column. It counts only what is drawn on this view, and is absent on a view that draws none. When the assistant's layer is hidden, a line says how many of them it hides.
  • ⋯, on the right, is the view's menu.
  • The side column button, a panel icon, shows or hides the side column.

The ⋯ menus ​

Every view keeps its own actions, options and filters behind one ⋯ button, so its head stays clear. A row may carry a key or a command name on its right, a tick when it is an option that is on, or red type when it removes something. Some menus have a small heading over a group of rows, such as Threads as, followed by Lanes and Arcs.

Rows in tables and cards carry their own ⋯ too. A menu closes when you choose a row, press Esc, or click elsewhere.

The frame's own menus (the home switcher, the story's ⋯ in the sidebar, the breadcrumb's story and view menus) work the same way and open over the sidebar and the title bar.

The side column ​

The side column sits at the right edge of a view, beside it, never on top of it, so nothing you filter or edit is hidden by it. It holds the view's sections: filters, the selected scene or person, settings for the view. Each section is a heading you can fold, with its current value on the right so it reads without opening, such as 2 on or 4 of 5 shown.

Press Ctrl+B, click the panel icon in the head, or choose Toggle the side column from the View menu. Some actions open a sheet in the column's place, such as the proposals for a story; Ctrl+B then closes the sheet first. A sheet has a title, sometimes the file it writes in small type beside it, and an × to close it.

The footer and the keys of a view ​

The footer lists the keys this view answers to, such as Tab next name · ← → ↑ ↓ nearest name · Enter pin … · ? these keys in the Graph. Some views put their own item at its right end; the manuscript puts its lens there.

Press ? to open Keys of this view, a small panel listing each key and what it does. Esc or its × closes it. Its last line states the rules every view keeps:

  • dead in fields: single keys do nothing while you type in a field, so typing never runs a command;
  • Tab is never taken: Tab always moves between controls. The one exception is a review, where Tab from the page or from a proposal moves to the next proposal; from any other control it still moves between controls;
  • chords in the palette: every chord, such as Ctrl+4, is also a command in the find field.

Empty views ​

A view with nothing to show says why, and offers the fix as a button. When a view is empty because nothing was found in the story, such as a Graph with no notes, it adds Why? See how this story is read, which opens How this story is read.

The native menu ​

The menu bar (at the top of the screen on macOS, at the top of the window elsewhere) carries the same commands, with their keys:

MenuCommands

EditUndo, Redo, Cut, Copy, Paste, Paste and Match Style, Delete, Select All, worded by your system

On Windows and Linux a Help menu at the end holds About Leathercase, which opens the About page in Settings: the version, and a link to every version and its notes.

A view command chosen at a place of the home, with no story open, opens the story you worked on last. Every key can be changed: see Keys and commands.

Hints ​

Rest the pointer on a control that shows only an icon, and after a moment a small hint says what it does: Toggle the side column, Close the mate, Sidebar · Ctrl \. Controls whose name is cut short show their full name the same way. Moving along a row of buttons, the next hint follows quickly. A click, a key, scrolling or leaving the window puts the hint away. Hints appear in the app's own type, never as the system's tooltip.

Light and dark ​

Leathercase has a light theme and a dark theme, and by default follows your desktop's setting. To choose:

  • Settings, Appearance, Mode: Light, Dark or Match system.
  • Or run Theme: light, Theme: dark or Theme: follow the desktop from the View menu, or from the find field: type theme and pick the row, or type Theme: light in full and press Enter.

The theme changes the interface only. The meaning of a colour never changes with it: vermilion is yours, rose needs checking, dashed is read from the source, green is verified, amber is waiting. Settings, Appearance draws each of these marks beside its meaning.

Size, contrast and colour ​

Settings, Accessibility holds what changes how the app meets your eyes. Each is a preference of this computer.

  • Zoom grows or shrinks the whole window, words and controls together, from 50% to 300%. Ctrl+= and Ctrl+- do it from anywhere, in steps; Actual size in the View menu puts it back to 100%.
  • Prose size sets the manuscript's text on top of the zoom. It is the same setting as under Appearance.
  • Stronger contrast darkens the grey text and the borders, in both themes.
  • Colour-blind colours turns verified blue instead of green, and gives needs checking a diamond and waiting a triangle as well as their colours. It is made for red-green and blue-yellow colour blindness; what each mark means stays the same.
  • Reduce motion turns the fades off.

See Settings.

When something goes wrong ​

When an action fails, a rose line appears across the top of the view, under the title bar, saying what went wrong in a sentence, such as "Novel/Chapter 3.md" cannot be read, so Leathercase leaves it as it is rather than write over it. Mend it by hand, or wait for your sync to finish, then try again. Press its × (Dismiss) to put it away. A line with no × stays until what it names is fixed.

Steps of a journey (first run, declaring a story) report their problem in rose under the step instead, and wait for you to change your answer.

If Leathercase itself did not start cleanly, a system dialog says Leathercase did not start cleanly, with what went wrong. Restart the app.
