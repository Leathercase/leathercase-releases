Comments and word lists ​

This page covers two kinds of notes you keep beside your prose without writing them into it. Comments are remarks on a passage: your own, typed in your notes, and the ones you make on the manuscript page, which Leathercase keeps in a file of its own. Word lists are the words you want to watch, which the words lens marks on the page. Both live in plain markdown files you can open and edit without Leathercase.

Two kinds of comment ​

Every comment of a story sits in one list, but they come from two places:

Your commentsLeathercase's comments
WrittenBy you, in your notes, in your own appOn the manuscript page, in Leathercase
Kept inThe chapter itself, as %% … %% or ==…==Comments.md in the story's suite folder
Shown asin your notesuite
Resolved or removed from LeathercaseNo: change them in your noteYes

Leathercase reads your notes and never writes into them, which is why a comment made in Leathercase goes to a file beside the story and not into the chapter.

Your own comments in your notes ​

Leathercase reads two marks in your text:

  • %% a comment %%: a hidden comment, the kind Obsidian hides in reading view. It may span lines.
  • ==a highlight==: highlighted text. It shows highlighted on the page, and is listed as a mark.

A comment that opens with an upper-case word and a colon has that word as its kind, the way code editors colour TODO and FIXME:

markdown
%% TODO: the eight-second rhythm needs to be felt before it is explained %%
%% CHECK: was it a coat or a cape in chapter three? %%

The kind colours the comment: todo and fix in the accent colour (something to do), check in the warning colour (something to verify), cut struck through, and any other kind, or none, muted. A lower-case "check the gate" is prose, not a kind. A comment that ends with ✓ is resolved.

Chapter one of The Lamp at Ferrol carries one: %% TODO: the eight-second rhythm needs to be felt before it is explained %%.

Comments inside front matter or code blocks are not read. A comment of the form %% not cliche %% is an instruction to the style lens, and %% Tomás %% before a line of speech pins its speaker (see Lenses and style checks).

Making a comment on the page ​

  1. On the manuscript page, click a sentence (or drag over a passage, or move to a paragraph with the arrow keys).
  2. In the side column's Passage section, click Comment on this passage.
  3. The composer opens. Pick a Kind if you want one: no kind, todo, fix, check, idea, cut or ref.
  4. Type in Comment on this paragraph…. Enter adds it; Shift+Enter starts a new line. Or click Add.

The line under the field says what will happen: "enter adds it · shift+enter for a new line · written to Comments.md in your suite folder, anchored to "…"", with the start of the sentence it hangs on. With nothing selected the field is greyed and says Pick a paragraph on the page….

The comment is written to Comments.md, anchored to the sentence you selected, under the chapter and scene it is in. Your chapter is not touched. The status line at the foot of the page says Commented on ¶ 3 of The Night the Lamp Went Out, with Undo.

The comments list ​

All comments, the last section of the side column, lists every comment of the book in reading order. It is folded at first; its hint shows the count, or 2 open of 5 when some are resolved.

  • Chapter names head each group, with the chapter's count and how many are open (1 open, or all resolved).
  • Each comment shows its kind in lower case (check, todo, note when it has none, mark for a highlight), who made it (suite or in your note), its text, and the line of prose it hangs on in italics.
  • All and Open at the top switch between every comment and the open ones only.
  • Click a comment to go to its paragraph: the page scrolls there and selects it. The comment of the paragraph you are on is marked.

Leathercase's own comments carry two small buttons, shown when you point at the comment:

ButtonDoes
✓Resolve the comment: it stays, greyed, with a ✓ added in Comments.md. On a resolved comment the same button is Reopen.
×Remove the comment from Comments.md.

Both go on the page's undo stack. Your own comments have no buttons: change them in your note.

The comments on the paragraph you have selected are also listed in the Passage section, under the actions.

With no comment at all the list says: "No comment yet. Pick a paragraph and write one below it; it goes to Comments.md in your suite folder. Your own %% comments %% and ==highlights== in the notes are listed here too."

How a comment finds its place again ​

A comment of Leathercase's does not store a line number, which would break the first time you edited the chapter. It stores the chapter's name, the scene heading and the sentence you selected, and finds them again each time the story is read:

  1. The chapter, by its file name (with or without folders, in any case).
  2. The scene, by its heading, inside the chapter.
  3. The paragraph, by the quoted sentence, inside the scene. Punctuation, case and the kind of quote marks do not matter: a sentence quoted with straight, curly, single or double marks is the same sentence, so a quote never reads as sentence moved for its marks alone.

A sentence you moved to another scene of the same chapter is still found there. If you rewrite the sentence, the comment still shows, on the scene's first paragraph, marked sentence moved. A comment made on a scene as a whole sits on its first paragraph.

Comments.md ​

Comments.md is author data: a markdown file of Leathercase's, in the story's suite folder (The Lamp at Ferrol/suite/Comments.md for the sample, or the folder you chose under Leathercase's files go in in the story's settings). It is made with your first comment, and opens with a short paragraph saying what it is. Then one heading per scene, one line per comment:

markdown
## [[01 The Night the Lamp Went Out#The cottage]]
- check: "The lamp went out at ten past two, and Nora knew it before she was awake." — Slower here.
- todo: The lamp pays off in part two. ✓
  • The heading links the chapter and scene, so it opens as a link in Obsidian.
  • The kind comes first, in lower case, then a colon.
  • The quoted sentence is the anchor; the text after the dash is the comment.
  • A trailing ✓ resolves it.

You can edit the file by hand: add a line, change a kind, tick one off. Leathercase reads it again with the story. Undo from the page only writes the file back if it still holds what Leathercase wrote, so a change you made by hand is never overwritten.

Word lists ​

A word list is a set of words or phrases you want to see on the page: words to cut, words never to use, your stage directions, your filter verbs. The words lens marks them, each list in its colour (see Lenses and style checks).

Editing the lists on the settings page ​

Open the story's settings (the story's ⋯ menu, Story settings) and pick Words on the left. The section heads with the file it writes, WordLists.md, and the line "The words to watch. Each change is written at once to Leathercase's file beside the story."

Under The story's word lists:

  • Each list is a row, its name in its colour, its words as chips. Click the × on a chip to take that word out (Take felt out of Filtering).
  • To add, type the list's name in List (it suggests the lists you have; the placeholder is Never use), the word or phrase in Word or phrase (placeholder suddenly), and press Enter or click Add. A new list name makes a new list.

The note under the lists, "How each character speaks is kept on their own sheet, in the characters view.", points to the accents, which are not word lists.

Adding from the page ​

With the words lens on the manuscript page, drag across a word or short phrase and use Put “felt” in a word list; select a marked paragraph and use Take “felt” out of the word lists. See Lenses and style checks.

WordLists.md ​

WordLists.md sits in the story's suite folder, beside Comments.md. One heading per list, the words under it, separated by commas or one to a line:

markdown
## Filtering
felt, saw, heard, noticed

## Stage direction
colour: #7a4fd6
then, before, and then
  • A colour: #hex line under a heading fixes the list's colour. Without one, colours follow the order of the headings from Leathercase's palette.
  • Bullets are allowed. Words are matched without regard to case or curly apostrophes, and a phrase is matched as a whole.
  • Front matter, comments and code blocks are your own text: never read as words, and never where Leathercase writes.

Leathercase writes the file only when you add or take out a word, and keeps the rest of it, your own lines too, as you wrote them. The story's settings page lists it under Leathercase's files with the note "yours to write: a heading per category, the words under it" until it exists.
