Sentence log ​

Many writers keep a file of the sentences they cut, because deleting a good sentence is the hardest thing in revision. Leathercase keeps that file for you. Once per sitting it compares each sentence as it was when you sat down with what it became, and the sentences you rewrote or cut go into the story's sentence log, with where they were, the day, and what replaced them. You never have to save a copy before you cut. The log only reads your files; nothing in it is ever written back into a chapter.

What a sitting is ​

Leathercase cannot see your cursor; it sees your files each time you save them. So it compares a sentence once per sitting, not at every save:

  • A sitting begins when you open the story in Leathercase. The text at that moment is the starting point.
  • Each time you save in your writing app, Leathercase reads the story again and moves the sitting on. Nothing is logged yet, so a save in the middle of a word never logs anything, and a sentence rewritten three times in one sitting is logged once, as it was before you started.
  • The sitting ends when you close the story, or after half an hour with no change, or when the day turns. Then Leathercase compares the start with the end and logs what changed. An app left open for days still logs each day's work on its own day.

Work done while Leathercase was closed is not lost. Leathercase keeps, for each story, the text the last sitting ended with. When you open the story next, it compares that with the files as they are now and logs whatever changed in between, in any software, on that day. The same happens after a crash or a quit before the log was written.

Which scene you worked on ​

Each time Leathercase reads the story, it also notes which scenes changed and by how many words, including what you changed while it was closed. These notes go to the story's writing log, not to the sentence log. They are there so that an agent you connected can tell you the scene you worked on last. It says how it knows: from Leathercase's own record, or, for work from before Leathercase first read the story, from the date of the chapter's file. A change found when the story opens is noted as made some time after Leathercase last read it, since it cannot know when. See Files Leathercase writes.

What counts as a change ​

Not every edit is a revision. Leathercase logs a sentence only when it was rewritten or cut:

You didLogged?
Fixed a typo ("teh" to "the"), or changed only case or punctuationNo
Changed two words or more, or a quarter of a short sentence's lettersYes, as rewritten, with what replaced it
Deleted a sentence, or a whole paragraphYes, each sentence as cut
Moved a sentence to another place, in the same chapter or another oneNo: it was moved, not cut
Deleted a file whose sentences turn up nowhere elseYes, each sentence as cut

To tell a rewrite from a cut, Leathercase looks for the new sentence most like the old one nearby. If they share enough words in order, the new one replaced the old; if nothing is close enough, the old one was cut and the new one written fresh.

In the manuscript's side column ​

On the manuscript page, click a sentence. The side column's Sentence log section, under Passage, lists what was logged for that paragraph, newest first. Its hint gives the count, such as 2 sentences, and the section opens by itself when there is something to show.

Each entry shows:

  • The sentence as it was, struck through.
  • Rewritten Oct 6 or Cut Oct 6: what happened and the day.
  • Now: and the sentence that replaced it, for a rewrite.
  • Copy, which puts the old sentence on the clipboard. The button reads Copied after.

The line under the list says: "Copy puts a sentence on the clipboard. Leathercase never writes it back into your file." To restore a sentence, copy it and paste it in your own app.

A paragraph shows the sentences logged at its number in the chapter, and any rewrite whose new sentence is in it now, so a rewrite stays with its paragraph even after paragraphs above it were added or cut.

With nothing logged for the paragraph the section says: "Nothing logged here. A sentence you rewrite or cut in this paragraph is kept when the sitting ends."

On the Desk ​

The Desk counts the day's logged sentences as revision work. Under Revision work it lists each story with sentences logged today, such as The Lamp at Ferrol · 4 sentences logged today, with the line "Rewritten and cut sentences, kept in each story's sentence log." Click a story to open it. A day spent cutting and rewriting shows as work done, even when the word count went down.

Marking a moment ​

A mark names a point in the log, so you can find "everything I cut after the dinner rewrite" later. With a story open:

  1. Run Mark the sentence log from the command palette (Ctrl+K, or Cmd+K on macOS). The find field in the title bar opens with mark: typed in.
  2. Type the name of the moment, for example mark: before the rewrite.
  3. Pick Mark the sentence log: before the rewrite and press Enter.

You can also type mark: and a name into the find field (Ctrl/Cmd+O) yourself. Nothing changes on screen: the mark is written to the log, and in the log as rendered below it is a heading, ### before the rewrite, under the day's heading. Everything logged after it sits above it, since the log reads newest first.

What the log looks like ​

Rendered as markdown, which is how an agent you connected reads it, the log reads newest first, one heading per day, one entry per sentence:

markdown
## 2026-10-06

- [[Act I - The Dark Lamp/01 The Night the Lamp Went Out.md#The cottage|01 The Night the Lamp Went Out › The cottage]] ¶ 2
  - ~~She had slept under that light for twenty-two years.~~
  - She had slept under that light all her life.

### before the rewrite

Each entry says where the sentence was (the chapter, the scene and the paragraph), strikes the old sentence through, and gives what replaced it under it. A struck sentence with nothing under it was cut. A ### heading is a mark you named. The rendering opens with a short paragraph in the story's language.

Where it is kept ​

The log is one of Leathercase's logs, kept in the story's suite folder under suite/logs/sentences/, never among your chapters:

FileWhat it holds
suite/logs/sentences/2026-10-06.<machine>.jsonlThe log itself: one line per logged sentence or mark, one file per day and per computer.
suite/Sentences.jsonThe text the last sitting ended with, so the next sitting can compare. Never shown, and not a second copy of your book: only what the log compares.
suite/logs/writing/The writing log: the words added and cut at each reading, and the scenes that changed.

Each computer writes only its own file, so a story synced between a desktop and a laptop keeps one log without two machines ever writing the same file. The log is kept forever; nothing is pruned.

A Sentence log.md from an earlier version of Leathercase (and its yearly files, such as Sentence log 2026.md) is read once, its entries taken into the log, and then left as it was. Leathercase no longer writes Sentence log.md, and it never reads either file as part of your story.
