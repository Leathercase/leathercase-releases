Homes and stories ​

A writing home is the folder that holds your stories. This page says what a home is, what Leathercase keeps in it, how the Library shows its stories, how to have more than one home, how to remove one, and what happens when you rename a note in your own writing app.

Keys are written for Windows and Linux; on macOS use Cmd in place of Ctrl.

What a writing home is ​

A writing home is one folder on your computer that holds one story or many, in whatever shape you keep them: folders of markdown notes, an Obsidian vault, Word files, Scrivener projects, Fountain screenplays. You choose it at first run (see Getting started), and from then on Leathercase opens on it.

Leathercase reads the home and never writes into your files. It keeps its own files in the same folder, beside your stories, for one reason: whatever carries your writing (a backup, Dropbox, Google Drive, iCloud Drive, OneDrive) carries Leathercase's files too, and a second computer that opens the folder finds every story as you left it.

Inside a home, a story is a folder Leathercase has been told is a story. Telling it is called declaring; see Declaring a story. A folder of the home that was never declared is not read.

What a home holds ​

On diskWhat it isWho writes it
Suite.mdA note at the home's root that marks the folder as a writing home and lists its stories, where each lives and which files it reads.Leathercase writes the note once, then rewrites only the list of stories, between two marks. The rest of the note is yours to change. A Suite.md Leathercase did not write is left alone.
suite/The home's own structured files: its manifest (Library.json), the author and pen names, the Writer Board's layout, described tags (Tags.md), and the daily snapshots in suite/daily.Leathercase
Writer/Your cards from the Writer Board, one markdown note each, shared by every story in the home, and in Writer/Templates/ the Graph templates you change or make. Made only when you make your first card or template.Leathercase makes a card; what you write in it is yours
A suite/ folder inside each story's folderThat story's settings (Story.json), export settings, layouts and snapshots as JSON; its Graph as markdown in suite/Graph/ (the ties in Ties.md, a file for each tab); its writing and sentence logs as JSON Lines in suite/logs/, which also record when each scene last changed; and its threads (StoryThreads.md), plot grid (PlotGrid.md), comments and beat cards as markdown. All of it travels with the story's folder.Leathercase
Everything elseYour stories and notesYou. Leathercase reads it and never writes into it.

Every file Leathercase writes is a small markdown or JSON file you can open without Leathercase. A folder named suite is never read as prose, wherever it sits in the home. The full list is on Files Leathercase writes.

What belongs to this computer alone stays in the app's own data, never in the home: the list of homes you opened here, your preferences, the passage to resume, the connected agents. Removing a home clears that; see below.

The Library ​

The Library is the home's front page. Open it with Library in the sidebar, Ctrl+1, or by clicking the home's name at the start of the breadcrumb. It is also where Leathercase opens.

The header ​

The title Library, the number of stories (4 stories), and on the right New story, which opens the journey that declares a story. The same journey opens from the plus beside Stories in the sidebar and from Declare a story in the File menu.

Resume ​

When you last selected a passage in a story's manuscript, a card at the top brings you back to it. It reads Resume · The Lamp at Ferrol · The Ledger, then the passage itself in quotes, and when you selected it: selected today, 14:02, selected yesterday, 21:40, or the date.

  • Click the quote to open the manuscript at that passage.
  • Open in Obsidian (or the app the story opens in, or Open in your default app) opens the file in your writing app, at that spot where the app can go there.
  • Show in Plot grid opens the story's plot grid at that scene.

The card is absent until you select a passage, and goes when its story is no longer declared.

The table of stories ​

One row per story, the one you worked on most recently first; stories never read come last. Click a row or the story's name to open it, on the view you last used.

ColumnWhat it shows
StoryThe story's mark and name, and under it how many files it reads, or not read yet
SourceWhere it is read from: Markdown, Obsidian, Word, Scrivener or Fountain, told from its files. A dashed Not read before the first reading.
WordsHow many words the story holds now, or a dash before it is read
ChangedWhen its files last changed: today, yesterday, 3 days ago, last week, then the date
StateA dot, a word for where the story stands, and a button that takes you to the fix

The source is read from the files: Scrivener when a file sits in a .scriv project, otherwise the format most of the files are in; markdown inside an Obsidian vault is Obsidian.

The state compares what the story holds with the goal you set for it (a target in words, a deadline, a daily goal), set when you declare it or in its Story settings.

The same state colours the small dot at the foot of each story's mark in the sidebar, so you can see how every story stands from any view. Rest the pointer on a story's name and it says its state in lower case: on pace, no target. The sidebar's dot is quieter than the table's in three places: a story not read yet and a stalled story show grey there, and Open in not set is a question for the Library only, so the sidebar keeps showing the story's state.

In the sample, The Night Sleeper was given a 2,000-word target, which its 2,194 words meet.

A line at the foot repeats the promise: your files are read, never written · what Leathercase adds is markdown in a folder you choose.

A home with no stories ​

A home with no story says No stories in this home yet, explains what counts as one (a folder of markdown, a vault, a Scrivener project, Word files or a screenplay, inside your home's folder), and offers two buttons: New story, which opens the declare journey, and Show folder, which opens the home's folder in your system's file manager. A last line reminds you that the Writer library is shared by every story in the home and already open for ideas.

Start here ​

While the sample is open, a Start here column sits beside the Library. See Getting started.

More than one home ​

You can keep more than one home: one for a pen name, one for a series, one for poems. Each has its own stories, its own Writer Board and its own Desk.

Adding a home. Any of these opens your system's folder dialog, titled Open a library:

  • Add a writing home… in the home switcher at the top of the sidebar;
  • Add a writing home in the File menu, or in the find field;
  • Add a writing home… beside Other homes in Settings, Storage.

Pick the folder. Leathercase writes Suite.md and suite/ in it, adds it to this computer's list of homes and switches to it. A folder that is already a home, made on this or another computer, opens with its stories as they were. A new home starts with no stories: declare them from its Library. Adding a home does not walk you through first run again.

Some folders cannot be a home, and Leathercase says why: the root of a drive, your whole home folder (choose the folder inside it that holds your stories), a system folder, or the folder where Leathercase keeps its own data. A folder is known however its path is spelled, so the same folder is never listed twice.

If another home already has the folder's name, the new home is named apart, with the folder it sits in: a second storytelling inside Drafts becomes storytelling (Drafts). Settings opens at its name so you can give it one of your own.

Switching homes. Click the home switcher and pick another home; the open one is ticked and marked current. Every home shows its folder by its last two folders, such as …/Drafts/storytelling. Or use Switch beside a home in Settings, Storage, Other homes. Leathercase opens on the home you used last.

Naming a home. Choose Name and look… in the home switcher, or open Settings, Storage, This home. Type the home's name and press Enter, or leave the field; Esc puts the old name back. Pick a colour for its tile, Accent or one of nine, and an icon to show on it in place of the name's first letter, one of twenty-four, or First letter to go back. The name, colour and icon are kept in the home's suite/Library.json, so every computer the folder syncs to shows the home the same way. Renaming a home does not change Suite.md, which is yours to edit.

Settings, Storage ​

Settings, Storage describes the open home. Its file name in the corner is preferences.json, since most of what it lists is this computer's; the home's name and look are the exception, kept in suite/Library.json.

  • This home: the home's Name, Colour and Icon, kept in the home's folder. See Naming a home above.
  • Writing home: the folder's path, and Show folder to open it in your file manager.
  • Sync: who carries the folder. local with No sync tool holds this folder; it lives on this computer only., or detected with Dropbox carries this folder. Leathercase never uploads anything itself.
  • Daily snapshots: a copy of every suite folder, kept in suite/daily for 14 days, with how many days are kept, their size and the latest day. Manuscripts are never copied. Show opens the folder.
  • In this home: Suite.md, suite/, Writer/ and one suite/ per story, each with a line on what it holds.
  • Other homes: every other home on this computer, each with Remove… and Switch, or No other home when there is none. Add a writing home… sits beside the heading.
  • Remove this home: Delete writing home and Purge writing home, below.
  • When a sync tool made conflicted copies of Leathercase's files, a group lists them with Compare, Keep the copy and Keep this computer's. See FAQ and troubleshooting.

Removing a home ​

There are two ways to let a home go. Neither ever touches your prose.

DeletePurge
Takes the home off this computer's list of homesYesYes
Clears what this computer kept of it: the passage to resume, the capture target, the agents' access to it, the mate's memory of itYesYes
Moves Leathercase's files in the folder to the trashNoYes: Suite.md, every suite/ folder, and the cards Leathercase made in Writer/
Your manuscripts and your own notesStayStay
Opening the folder again laterTakes the home up as it wasStarts a new home

Delete ​

  1. Open Settings, Storage.
  2. Beside Delete writing home, press Delete…. For another home, press Remove… beside it under Other homes.
  3. The dialog, Delete Stories?, says that the home leaves this computer's list of homes with what this computer kept of it, and that nothing in the folder changes.
  4. Press Delete home, or Cancel.

Deleting the open home lands you on your next home, or on first run when it was the only one. Deleting another home leaves you where you are.

Purge ​

  1. Open Settings, Storage, and press Purge… beside Purge writing home. Or open the Delete dialog and choose Purge in the switch at its top.
  2. The dialog, Purge Stories?, reads the folder (Reading the folder…), then lists under Moves to the trash every file and folder Leathercase made there, for example Suite.md, suite/, The Lamp at Ferrol/suite/ and the cards in Writer/. When Leathercase made nothing there it says so.
  3. When a sync tool carries the folder, a rose note warns that Dropbox syncs this folder: these files will leave every device that syncs it.
  4. Under Left where they are, it lists folders you chose elsewhere for a story's notes from Leathercase. They may hold your own notes too, so Leathercase leaves them; delete what you no longer want by hand. Any other folder named suite that Leathercase did not make is left too, and so is any note in Writer/ that Leathercase did not write.
  5. Type Stories to confirm: type the home's name exactly. Purge home stays greyed out until you do.
  6. Press Purge home.

Everything goes to your system's trash, not straight to deletion, so you can take it back from there.

The sample ​

The sample is a home too. Leave the sample, in the home switcher or at the foot of Start here, deletes it the same way and also removes its copy from the app's data. See Getting started.

Uninstalling ​

Uninstalling the app leaves every home as it is, Leathercase's files included, on purpose. Purge each home first if you want them gone. See Updates and uninstalling.

A story whose folder moved ​

If you move or rename a story's folder, or its drive is not mounted, the Desk says The story's folder was not found, with The Lamp at Ferrol · moved, renamed or not mounted, and offers Find it. Press it and pick the folder where the story is now, inside the same home. The story's settings travel inside its suite/ folder, so Leathercase takes the story up from there with everything you set. While the folder is missing, the heartbeat of an open story says the folder cannot be read and the views show the last reading. See The Desk.

Renamed notes ​

A character, place, item, faction or event is known by its note, and its name is the note's file name. So when you rename Characters/Nora.md to Characters/Nora Vidal.md in your own app, Leathercase first sees one note gone and one new. It compares each reading with the last, and when the change looks like a rename it asks you.

The question ​

Over the story's views, a strip asks one rename at a time:

Nora looks renamed to Nora Vidal: its note's text is the same. Should Leathercase's own files follow the new name?

The reason given is one of three:

  • its note's text is the same;
  • the new note keeps Nora as another name (in its aliases:);
  • it is the only note gone and the only one new in its folder.

When more renames wait, the strip says 2 more wait. The same questions are listed under What looks off on How this story is read.

Yes, follow the new name ​

Yes, follow Nora Vidal makes Leathercase's own files follow the new name:

  • the Graph's ties in Graph/Ties.md and the roles in its tabs, and the relationships drawn on the old story map, in Relationships.md;
  • the accents in Accents.md and the threads in StoryThreads.md;
  • Leathercase's own note beside hers, which becomes suite/Characters/Nora Vidal.md;
  • any thread strip you hid.

Story.json keeps "Nora" as another name for Nora Vidal, so the prose that still says Nora finds her. The question goes. Your notes are not written: the rename you made is the only change to them.

No, they are different ​

No, they are different leaves Leathercase's files as they were, and Leathercase never asks about that pair again.

Names Leathercase's files still use ​

When one of Leathercase's files names someone no note of the story answers to any more (after a rename you said no to, or a note you deleted), How this story is read lists it: Relationships.md names Nora, and no note of the story answers to that name. It offers Open Relationships.md, or Open the story's settings when the name is in Story.json.
