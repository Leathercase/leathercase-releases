Threads ​

The Threads view shows your story's threads across the whole book, two ways. Lanes give each thread a row and mark every scene it touches, so you see when a thread goes quiet. Arcs lay the manuscript along one line and draw every place the book refers back to itself as an arc above it, as tall as the distance it spans. The threads are the same ones the plot grid shows as columns, kept in StoryThreads.md, so a stop added here is a cell there, and the other way round.

Opening the view ​

Open a story, then pick Threads under the story's name in the sidebar, press Ctrl+5 (Cmd+5 on macOS), or type "Threads" in the command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K).

The view opens on the lanes. To switch, open the view's ⋯ menu (Threads menu) and choose Lanes or Arcs under Threads as. The choice holds while the app is open.

Where threads come from ​

A thread is a ## heading in StoryThreads.md, in the story's author data folder (for The Lamp at Ferrol, The Lamp at Ferrol/suite/StoryThreads.md). Each list line under it is a stop: the scene it touches, then an optional role, a word from the thread's scale, a quoted sentence as its anchor, and a note.

markdown
## Subplot: The lens
- [[01 The Night the Lamp Went Out#The tower]] — plant: "One prism was cracked. One prism was missing." the gap in the four hundred
- [[08 The Lens#The beam]] — payoff: "Nineteen miles." the beam gathers

A quote of speech that ends at a comma takes the rest of its sentence with it. In — payoff: "Pilot on watch," Nora said. the boat comes in, the anchor is the whole sentence, "Pilot on watch," Nora said., as the prose has it, and the note is only the boat comes in.

The heading's prefix gives the thread its kind: Arc: for a character, Theme: for an argument the book makes, Subplot: for a line of events, nothing for a free thread. You can write the file by hand, add columns and cells on the plot grid, or add stops from the arcs chart (below). If the story's folder holds a Story threads.md from Creative Writer at its root, as the sample does, Leathercase reads it until its own file exists: the first change you make writes suite/StoryThreads.md and leaves the old file where it is, unchanged (see Files Leathercase writes). The full grammar is on the plot grid page.

Columns that carry a job on the grid (POV, Time and Plot point) describe a scene rather than refer back, so neither lanes nor arcs draw them.

Lanes ​

The lanes list your threads grouped by kind under Arcs, Themes, Subplots and Threads, each with its number of stops. Scenes run left to right in the grid's order, act and chapter names in a band across the top. On each lane:

  • A mark at every scene the thread stops in, by its role: plant, touch, reversal or payoff. A stop with no anchored quote is drawn as a plan.
  • A line from the first stop to the last: the thread's span.
  • An amber flag over a long silence, a run of scenes where the thread is not touched, labelled such as 6 scenes silent. A silence counts once it is a sixth of the story's scenes, and never fewer than five.

Hover a mark to read the scene and the stop's note. The Legend section in the side column names the marks.

Under the lanes, the axis runs from scene 1 to the last scene's number. When you last selected a passage in the manuscript, a dashed line marks its scene, and the axis says "dashed line: the last passage selected".

The state line counts threads, scenes and flagged silences, for example 5 threads · 20 scenes · 1 silence of 5 scenes or more.

Picking a lane ​

Click a lane, or walk them with ↑ and ↓. The other lanes dim. The side column shows the Thread section: its name, its summary (the comment line under its heading), and a line such as "scenes 1–19 · 4 stops · 4 verified". Under it, Stops lists each stop with its role, its chapter, its scene and its audit mark (plan, verified or broken). Click a stop to open its scene in the manuscript; Enter opens the first stop's.

When the lane has a silence, a Longest silence section says how long it runs and between which scenes, such as "6 scenes, from The road to The vestry.", and for an arc, how many of those scenes the character is still on stage in. Show those scenes on the grid goes to the plot grid. Esc clears the selection.

With nothing picked, the Thread section says "Click a lane to read its stops and its silences. Lanes show when each thread is touched across the story; Arcs show which passage points back at which."

With no threads yet, the view says "No threads yet. A thread is a heading in StoryThreads.md, or a column added on the Plot grid; its stops are the scenes it touches."

Arcs ​

The arcs chart draws the manuscript along one axis: a bar per scene, as wide as its words, with the scene's name over it where it fits and the chapter's name under it. Above the axis, every reference the book makes back to itself is an arc from one scene to another, as tall as its span.

Three kinds of reference, one line style each, named in the legend over the chart:

KindLineWhat it is
yoursStrong, in the accent colourYour own threads from StoryThreads.md: an arc between each stop and the next. Always drawn.
mentionFaintWhere a name of the story is mentioned, scene after scene. Off until you turn it on.
echoDottedA phrase said again, or two sentences that resemble each other, in two scenes. Off until you turn it on.

On your own threads:

  • promise → payoff: a thread with a plant and a payoff carries an arrow from the promise to what keeps it.
  • plant, not paid off: a plant that nothing pays off yet draws an arc that runs to nothing.
  • needs checking: a rose ring with ! over a scene marks a stop to check (see Needs checking). Click it to open that section.

The legend ends with the zoom and the story's word count, such as "fit · 5,120 words" or "2.0× · 5,120 words".

Hover an arc and its thread lights up. The strip at the foot of the view says "Click an arc to read it, a bar to see the scene."

Zoom ​

The chart fits the window at first. Zoom in with + (or =), out with -, or hold Ctrl (Cmd on macOS) and scroll the wheel over the chart; it stretches sideways only, up to eight times, and scrolls. F or 0 fits the manuscript again. The ⋯ menu has Zoom in, Zoom out and Fit the manuscript. Zoom is not remembered.

Reading an arc ​

Click an arc (or focus it and press Enter) to select it. Clicking the empty sky above the axis, or Esc, clears it. The side column's Selected section shows:

  • The thread's name and its kind: yours, a mention or an echo, with "promise → payoff" for a thread that has one.
  • "between The tower and The vestry", or, for a dangling plant, "a plant at The tower that nothing pays off yet".
  • The thread's summary, if it has one.
  • The two stops at the arc's ends: each with its role (or named for a mention, said for an echo), the scene (click to open it in the manuscript), its quote (marked "no longer in the scene" when the prose moved), its note, and the scene's number.
  • For your own threads: Promise makes the first stop a plant, Pays off makes the second a payoff, remove on a stop takes it out of the thread, and Go to scene opens the scene. A line says where the thread lives, such as StoryThreads.md · ## Subplot: The ledger · line 41.
  • For a mention or an echo: Make it a thread writes it into StoryThreads.md as a thread of yours, with a stop at every scene it touches (an echo keeps its sentences as quotes), and Go to scene.

Adding a scene to a thread ​

Click a scene's bar to select it (double-click to open it in the manuscript). The Scene section shows its name, its position ("4 of 20"), its file and word count, and Threads through here with each thread's role at this scene; click a thread to select its arc. With none it says "No thread passes through this scene yet."

Under Add to a thread, type or pick a thread in Existing thread or a new name, write a Note, optional, and click Add. The stop is written to StoryThreads.md; a new name starts a new thread. Go to scene opens the scene in the manuscript.

If nothing can be drawn, the chart says No arcs to draw and why: "No hand-drawn thread runs between two scenes yet.", or "StoryThreads.md has no threads in it yet.", with how many mentions and echoes are switched off and buttons Show mentions and Show echoes, "or click a scene bar below and add it to a thread". A story with no scene that has prose says "No scene with prose yet. Threads run across scenes; write one and they appear."

Kinds ​

The Kinds section counts each kind. Yours is always drawn. Mentions and Echoes each have an eye with their count: click it to draw that kind or hide it. The choice is remembered for the story.

You do not have to turn a whole kind on. Follow one name… draws only the mentions of the name you type or pick, and Follow one echo… draws only the phrase you pick. While you follow one, the eye is half filled, and a reset link beside the state line stops following.

How close two passages must be to count as an echo is the Echo sensitivity setting (Low, Medium, High) in Settings, shared with the manuscript's echo lens. Echoes are counted each time the story is read and never stored.

Strips ​

Under the axis run up to four strips, a bar per scene:

StripCounts
Cast on stagePeople on stage in the scene
First appearancesNames that appear for the first time
Threads throughThreads that touch the scene
Open promisesPromises the reader is carrying: from each plant to the payoff that keeps it, or to the end of the book when none does

Hover a bar for its value. The Strips section (closed at first, its title reading "4 of 4 shown") has an eye per strip, with its highest value, to show or hide it. Hidden strips are remembered for the story.

The gauge band ​

When a column of the plot grid has a scale and the gauge is shown, the same lanes draw as a band under the strips, read along the chart's scenes: each scene's charge, the running total or the scene-to-scene line, and the inversions or turns. Show the gauge / Hide the gauge in the ⋯ menu, and the Gauge section in the side column, are the grid's own: changing them here changes them on the grid. See Plot grid, the gauge.

Needs checking ​

The Needs checking section lists every stop of your threads that needs a look, and opens by itself when there is one. The state line counts them ("20 scenes · 31 arcs · 2 need checking"). Each finding carries its fixes in place:

FindingWhat it meansFixes
points at no sceneThe stop's link names no scene of the story (a renamed heading, a typo).Point at a scene… then Which scene? to repoint it; Drop the stop.
quote not in the sceneThe stop's quoted sentence is no longer in its scene.Pick the line again… then Which sentence? to choose from the scene's sentences; Drop the quote.
pays off before its promiseA payoff comes earlier in the book than the plant it keeps.Go to scene.

With nothing to check it says "Every stop points at a scene, every quote is still in its scene, and every payoff comes after its promise."

Undo ​

Every write from the chart (a stop added, a role set, a stop removed, a thread made, a stop repointed or requoted) is one change to StoryThreads.md, named in the strip at the foot, such as Added The quay to The ledger, with Undo and Redo. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes and Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z redoes; the strip then says "Undid: …" or "Redid: …". If StoryThreads.md was changed outside Leathercase since, undo writes nothing and the strip says "The file changed outside Leathercase since; nothing was written, and the step was dropped.", as on the plot grid. Your edit is never wiped. The lanes only read.

Proposals from agents ​

With the assistant's layer on (Ctrl/Cmd+.), threads an agent proposed are listed with the view until you accept or dismiss them. See Proposals and the inbox.
