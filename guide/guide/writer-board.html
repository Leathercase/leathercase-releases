The Writer Board ​

The Writer Board sits above every story. It holds what you are made of as a writer: the themes you keep arguing about, the people you keep writing, the premises waiting for a home, the quotes and research that outlive one book. It is a panel of lists you pan and zoom, with your stories and your ideas first and the lists of a framework below. This page covers every part of it: the lists, the cards, dragging, links and lines, the side column, and changing the lists to your own.

Nothing on the board is prose. A card the board makes is a small markdown note Leathercase owns; a note of yours that carries a board tag is shown and never written. Nothing on the board is sent to a model.

Open the board ​

Click Writer under Home in the sidebar, or press Ctrl+3 (Cmd+3 on macOS). The board belongs to the writing home, not to one story, so it is there whichever story you were in.

The line at the top of the board counts what is on it: 6 cards · 4 stories · 1 idea · Truby, then any filter in force, then the zoom, such as 80%. The board's menu, ⋯ at the top right (Writer menu), holds the zoom, the lines and the filters.

The panel ​

The board is a panel larger than the window. The lists stay where the framework puts them; you move the view:

  • Drag the background to pan.
  • Scroll the wheel to zoom in and out around the pointer, or use Zoom in and Zoom out in the menu, or + and -.
  • Fit in the menu, or f, shows the whole board.

The first time you open the board, it opens at the width of its widest band, top first, so the lists can be read. After that it opens where you left it; the view is kept in the home's suite/Writer.json.

The bands and lists ​

The board reads top to bottom in bands, each band a row of lists:

  • Stories and ideas, the first band, holds two fixed lists: Stories and Ideas.
  • The framework's bands follow. The default framework is Truby's, with the bands Wish list, Premises, Inspirations, References and Voices.
  • Unsorted comes last, and only when it holds something.

Each list has a head with its colour, its name and how many cards it holds, then the tag a note needs to land in it, such as #writer/theme. An empty list shows its hint, which teaches what to collect, and make a card here, or tag a note #writer/theme.

Stories ​

Stories holds one card per story declared in the home. It is not a list you add to: declare a story and its card appears. With no story yet, it says The stories declared in this library, each a card that opens the desk.

A story card shows:

  • The story's name, and its stage with a coloured dot: idea, in development, drafting, revising, finished or shelved.
  • Its premise, or No premise yet.
  • Its voice, the card named in the story's note as writing-voice, or voice none yet.
  • Its words, against its target when it has one, such as 18,420 of 60,000 words.
  • How many are in the cast, and the day you last worked on it.

The stage and premise come from writing-stage and writing-premise in the story's note, and the voice from writing-voice (see Scaffold a story folder and Front matter). A fourth key, writing-idea, names the premise card the story grew from; a premise card claimed that way, or one whose own writer-story names the story, counts as having found its home. The keys are read every time the board is drawn and never copied into the story's settings. With no stage written, Leathercase infers one: finished when the story holds its target, drafting when it has prose, in development before that.

Click a story card to select it; double-click it to open the story.

Ideas ​

Ideas is the list every board has, whatever the framework: story ideas waiting for a home, a what-if, a title, a scene that arrived first. A card with nowhere else to go lands here: plain text made from the find field, and every line captured from another app (see Capture a line from any app). Its cards are of the kind idea.

Unsorted ​

A note tagged under writer/ whose tag names no list, such as #writer/wildcard, sits in Unsorted, as do the cards of a list you removed. Nothing is lost: give the note a tag of a list, or add a list with that name, and it moves.

Cards are notes ​

A card on the board is one of two things, and the board treats them differently.

A card the board madeA note of yours
Where it livesThe home's Writer/ folderAnywhere in the home
How it joins the boardMade in a list, from the find field or by captureCarries a tag under writer/, in its front matter (tags: [writer/theme]) or in its text (#writer/theme)
What the board may doRewrite its title, text, kind, tags and references; retag it when you move itRead it, show its opening, place it on the board. Never write it
How it looksA plain cardA card marked your note

A card the board made is author data: a markdown note you can open, edit or move in any program. Writer/What we owe.md reads:

markdown
---
tags: [writer/theme, sea]
kind: theme
created: 2026-10-06
---
The drowned.

## References
- [[The keeper who stays]]

The first tag says which list it sits in; the others are its own tags. kind: is what the card is. The body is its text, and the cards it references are links under References. A note in the Writer/ folder that lacks the list tag or the created: date is treated as yours, and only read.

You can choose another folder for the cards: see Where the cards go.

Make a card ​

Click + a card at the foot of any list except Stories and Unsorted. A field opens with the placeholder # A title and What the card says, with [[links]] to other cards.

  • The first line is the card's title, written as a heading or not.
  • Enter on a one-line draft makes the card. Shift+Enter starts the text on the next line; once the draft has more than one line, Ctrl+Enter makes the card.
  • The button reads Add and then Add "What we owe" as you type. Cancel or Esc lets the draft go.
  • A #tag typed in the title or the text leaves it and becomes one of the card's tags, shown as a chip: Lamp #mystery makes the card Lamp, tagged mystery.
  • [[Title]] in the text links to another card and draws a line to it.

The card takes the kind of its list (see the table above) and lands at the top of the list.

You can also make a card without leaving the keyboard: press n to open the field in the selected card's list, or in Ideas, or press Ctrl+O and type theme: What we owe in the find field. See Find, open and make.

Select a card and the side column ​

Click a card to select it; click it again, press Esc, or click close in the column to let it go. Double-click a card of the board's to edit it in place (see below); double-click a note of yours to open it in the program you write in.

A card shows the opening of its text, up to ten lines, each line as written, so a poem keeps its lines. Anything longer ends in an ellipsis; the whole text is in the column.

The side column (Ctrl+B shows or hides it) shows what is selected under Selected:

  • The title and the file's path.
  • The text, for a card of the board's, in a field you can edit. It is saved when you leave the field, press Ctrl+Enter, or leave the board, never on every key, so one edit is one write. For a note of yours, its opening is shown, up to ten lines.
  • Kind: a list of the board's kinds and untyped, for a card of the board's. For a note of yours, its kind as its kind: line says (a character, by its kind: line), or untyped; kind: in the note's front matter types it.
  • Tags: the card's own tags as chips. On a card of the board's, × takes a tag off, and the field a tag, new or known with + a tag puts one on, offering the tags the board knows. your note's tags are read, never written on a note of yours.
  • tagged #writer/theme, and placed in a list when the card was moved on the board alone.
  • Which stories use it: in no story yet, in The Lamp at Ferrol, or recurs in two or more. A story uses a card when a note of the story links to it, [[What we owe]], or names it in a comment, %% REF: [[What we owe]] %%, which stays out of the manuscript and any export. A card that recurs is the simplest picture of what you keep returning to.
  • Open note for a note of yours, and Put back by its tag for a card you placed in another list.
  • Move to, a list of the board's lists (see below).
  • References, for a card of the board's: the cards it references, each with drop, and Reference cards…, which lists the board's other cards with the ones referenced ticked. Find a card narrows the list; tick and untick as many as you like and Reference 3 cards writes them all at once, under the card's References heading. Or, with the card selected, shift+click cards on the board: each one is referenced, or dropped when it already was.
  • The lines the card has with other cards, each naming the card at the other end and whether it links. Click a name to select that card. With none, it says no line yet; reference a card above, or for a note of yours, no line yet; a [[link]] in the note draws one.

A selected story shows its stage, words and cast, its premise (or No premise yet. Put writing-premise on the project note and it shows here.), its voice, and Open at the desk, which opens the story.

Edit a card in place ​

Double-click a card of the board's, or select it and press Enter, and the card becomes the same field you made it in: the first line is its title, the rest its text.

  • Ctrl+Enter or Save saves it, and so does clicking anywhere outside the card, on the board or another card, which also closes it. Esc puts the card back as it was.
  • A new title renames the card's file: Writer/What we owe.md becomes Writer/What we keep.md. The card keeps its list, its place and the cards it references. A title another card has is refused, with Another card is called … under the field; clicking outside then puts the card back as it was.
  • A #tag typed in the title or the text becomes one of the card's tags, as when you made it.
  • A card that references this one by its old title, [[What we owe]], is not rewritten: it loses its line to the card until you reference the new title.
  • A note of a story that links the card by its old title is yours, so it is not rewritten either: the card stops counting that story until you change the link.

A note of yours is never edited on the board: double-click opens it in the program you write in.

Move a card ​

Drag a card to another list, or up and down within one; it lands where you let go. m puts the caret in Move to in the column, which moves the selected card to the foot of the list you pick.

What moving does depends on whose card it is:

  • A card of the board's is retagged: dragging What we owe from Themes to Craft rewrites its list tag from writer/theme to writer/craft in its note. Its text and its other tags stay.
  • A note of yours moves on the board alone. The note keeps its tag as written; the board remembers the move in suite/Writer.json and marks the card placed · tag: theme. Put back by its tag returns it to the list its tag names.

The order you give cards within a list is kept too. A card tagged for two lists shows in both, with also and the other list's name; dragging moves the copy you took.

Links and lines ​

A line on the board joins two cards that link to each other: a [[link]] in either card's text or references.

Lines at the board's bottom left says which lines are drawn, and the menu's Lines section and e change it too. Leathercase remembers the choice on this computer.

  • Card, the default: hover a card and its lines are drawn; select it and they stay while it is selected.
  • All draws every line of the board at once; the top line says every line.
  • Off draws none; the top line says lines off. The column still lists the selected card's lines.

A line leaves one card's edge and ends in an arrow at the card it links to; the lines of the card in hand are drawn in the accent colour. Hovering or selecting a card dims the cards it is not joined to, whatever the lines setting.

A link in a card's text is underlined. Click it and the board selects the card with that title and moves it to the middle of the view. A link to a note that is not on the board opens the note in the program you write in.

Each card shows how many lines it has, 2 lines, at its foot.

Filter by kind or tag ​

The menu's Kind section has Every kind and each kind on the board; its Tag section has Every tag and each tag the cards carry. Pick one and every card without it dims; the top line says theme shown or #sea shown. A tag counts the tags nested under it, so #sea lights cards tagged #sea/charts too. Pick Every kind or Every tag to clear it.

A tag given a colour in the Tags view shows in that colour on every card.

Kinds ​

A kind says what a card is, whichever list it sits in: a character, a place, an event, a theme, a premise, a reference or an idea. A card made in a list takes the list's kind, or else the first kind of its band; Ideas makes ideas; Unsorted makes none. You can change a card's kind in the column at any time; only its kind: line changes.

Each band offers a set of kinds, listed in the column under Lists. To add one, type it in A kind of your own, pick the band, and click + a kind. To take one from a band, click the × beside it. Cards of that kind keep it as written, and a list that made cards of it makes them of the band's first kind instead. A band with no kinds says none; its cards are untyped.

Every kind on the board is also a word in the find field: add motif and motif: The bell makes a card.

Change the lists ​

The column's Lists section names the framework and explains: Stories and Ideas are fixed. The rest are Truby's lists in its bands, yours to rename, remove and add to; the first change makes the framework your own copy. After the first change, the section reads Truby, yours and the top line says Truby, yours.

  • Add a list: type its name in A list of your own, pick the band, or a new band, and click + a list. Its tag is made from the name, so Smells takes #writer/smells; a new band takes the list's name.
  • Rename a list: click the list's head to select it. The column shows its tag, its card count and its hint, a field with its name and Rename. The tag stays the same, so every card stays in it.
  • Remove a list: select it and click Remove the list. Its cards go to Unsorted with their tags as written; a list with nothing on it goes quietly. The last list of the board cannot be removed.

Stories, Ideas and Unsorted cannot be renamed or removed.

Where the cards go ​

The column's last section, Your cards go in, shows the folder the board writes its cards to, Writer at the home's root by default. Choose… picks another folder in the home; the cards then go in a Writer folder inside it. Use the default returns to the home's root. Each card you make is a markdown note in that folder, tagged for its list. Your other notes are read and never written.

Keys ​

Press ? on the board to see its keys. Esc puts the list away.

KeyWhat it does

What the board writes ​

FileWhat it holds
Writer/*.mdThe cards the board made, one note each
suite/Writer.jsonYour own copy of the framework once you change it, the lists you moved your notes to, the order of cards in each list, and the view

The cards' folder also holds Templates/, where the Graph keeps the templates you change or make. Those notes carry no board tag, so they never show as cards.

The board never writes to a note of yours. When there is nothing on it, it says so: Nothing on the board yet. Make a card in any list and it becomes a note in your folder; tag a note of your own and it appears here on the next reading. Your notes are read, never written.
